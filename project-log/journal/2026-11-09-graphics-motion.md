## 2026-11-09, Graphics 3: the GPU graphics stage and motion (slice 3 of D129)

- Built slice 3 on `graphics-motion`: pvj-render (0.11.0) draws a graphic with the GPU, and the panel gives each graphic a motion. Where the player has the stage a graphic is a slot (1 to 8, the order of the list); the picture the rasteriser made is loaded once and the renderer works out a fade, a typewriter, a scroll or a crawl every frame from its own clock, so the panel sends nothing while a motion runs. On classic mpv the same pictures are drawn with `overlay-add` as in slice 2, with the fade and the typewriter stepped about twelve times a second by a thread that has a finite job.
- **Where it sits in the frame (the design left this to the builder).** `final_pass` in `render/src/main.c` draws the picture (the mapping's shader or the plain scale) and then the text layer. `gfx_draw` is called between the two: after the mapping, so a surface never warps a graphic, and under the text layer, so the masks (30) and the pairing codes (40 to 43) stay on top, as on classic. The captures and the screenshots use the same pass and show the graphics. The graphics are laid out in fractions of the screen, so the 720-line final pass of D108 scales them with the picture.
- **The renderer** (`render/src/gfx.c`, the `pvj-graphic` command and the `pvj-graphics` property in `ipc.c`): `load N PATH W H LINES` (an absolute regular file, never a link, W x H of premultiplied B G R A, a side no larger than the GPU's texture size and 4096, all pictures together at most 48 MB, loaded and waiting), `set N {x, y, motion, seconds, out, speed, loop, opacity}` (all or nothing, every number checked, NaN refused), `show`, `hide`, `replay`, `clear`. A client's thread reads the file and measures each line's ink before it takes `app.lock`; under the lock it only swaps pointers; the render thread uploads at most two pictures a frame (a texture swizzle reads B G R A, so nothing is turned on the CPU) and takes the lock once a frame to work out every slot. No new lock, and the order fx_lock, layer_lock, app.lock is kept (the new handlers take only `app.lock`). `pvj-render` gains `graphics` and `graphics-side`, which is how the panel knows a renderer has the stage (`Player.graphics_stage()`, asked once per player process like `fades_native`).
- **Motion in the shader**: fade (alpha from the clock; a hide with an out time keeps it drawn while it falls from where it was, so a hide half way through a fade in goes down from there), typewriter (the picture has `LINES` equal bands; each band's ink is measured at load; the bands are revealed one after the other at one pace along the ink with a soft edge, so letters do not shift), scroll (from below the screen to above) and crawl (from the right edge to the left), once or looped, positions not snapped so a slow one is smooth. A scroll or a crawl that has run off is done until Replay.
- **A crawl is one wide line, and a long one is refused in words**: the rasteriser takes `nofit 1` (a new request word; it refuses a text it would have had to make smaller, "Too long for this node's ticker at this size: shorten it or lower the size."), the width limit is the chip's texture size from `graphics-side`, and the refusal is the graphic's status, not retried for the same picture.
- **The classic path**: `mpv`'s `osd-overlay` cannot animate (slice 2's spike), so the fade and the typewriter are stepped here: each step writes a faded copy (`bytes.translate`, a table scaling every byte, which keeps it premultiplied) or a part-typed copy of the picture and `overlay-add`s it, about 12 times a second, and the last step is the picture's own file. Bounded: only a picture of 6 MB or less is stepped (else shown at once), 12 MB of pictures at once, every motion has an end (at most 30 s), the thread waits when nothing runs, a hide stops the steps and takes the overlay off (a fade out is stepped down first), and the step files are removed as they are replaced. Scroll and crawl are refused in words ("Scroll and crawl need the New renderer. This node draws graphics without the New renderer: only None, Fade and Typewriter, with steps.") and a chosen one is kept and drawn still, saying so.
- **Falling back and coming back**: the mode is looked at each pass and each tick; if it changes the old way's state is forgotten and the new way draws everything again from the same picture files. `_once` remembers what had been shown, so a node that falls back to classic mid-show shows the static graphic and plays nothing again (when the renderer comes back, or is restarted, each graphic's motion starts as a show does). Graphics are still held while Access is on the screen (the slots are cleared and the pictures stay built, so they come back without being made again); the pin screen now calls `gfx.watch()` itself when the Owner shows or hides a code, outside its own lock, so the hold is not a tick late.
- **Panel**: Motion in each graphic's settings (None, Fade, Typewriter, Scroll, Crawl, the unavailable ones greyed out with the reason; fade in and out times; the typing time; speed and loop), Replay beside Show; a new line sent to a graphic that is on plays its motion again; `POST /api/graphics {id, replay: true}`.
- Checked here: `tests/test_graphics_gpu.py` (new, the real renderer headless on llvmpipe: placement to the pixel, premultiplication and B G R order, order of slots and the text layer over them, fade in, out and from the middle, typewriter, scroll, crawl, loop, refusals, the texture limit with a test hook, the 48 MB limit, clear gives the memory back, four clients at once, two loads of one slot at once, the output rate with four graphics on), `tests/test_graphics_motion.py` (new: the panel's side on both paths with a fake player and a clock), `tests/test_graphics.py` (four expectations changed for motion), `tests/test_raster.py` (nofit), `tests/ui/panel.test.js` (the Motion controls on both kinds of node). The renderer is built with `WERROR=1`. ThreadSanitizer on the whole of the new test file (26 tests): no report touches `gfx.c` or the `pvj-graphic` handlers; the reports there are the ones libgallium and libmpv give on every run and the existing shutdown order of `layer_close` and `event_loop`.
- **Review fixes (same branch).** A lost answer from the player is no longer a fall back: `Player.graphics_stage()` raises when the player did not answer (and is None only when mpv itself says it has no stage), the engine keeps the last stage it was told, and leaving the GPU path sends a best-effort `clear` for every slot so a renderer that is still there stops drawing under the overlays. `gfx_load` looks at the budget before it allocates or reads; a slot counts the larger of its texture and its newest picture; a refused upload gives its bytes back; a frame with nothing waiting and nothing loaded takes no lock and does no work (an atomic flag and a count). A graphic comes back from a PIN or pairing code hold as it was on both paths (`show ... still` on the GPU, a whole picture on classic; a scroll or crawl just goes on), and Replay plays it. A show during a fade out starts at the level the fade out had reached (done in the renderer, so the docs need no caveat). Moving a graphic in the list still replays its motion and a fade out already running finishes under a hold: both are said in PANEL.md and the manual. The pace test judges the vblanks missed in its own window against the flips (at most a quarter), not a count since the start, so a slow runner has room while the flip rate is still held to 85 percent of 60.
- **Not measured on hardware.** node-01 has pvj-render 0.10.0 installed and nothing was deployed, so no frame-cost number exists; the targets of design 3.2 stand. The rows are in `tools/DEVICE-TESTING.md` (GM1 to GM15), all NOT YET RUN.
