## 2026-11-09, Node hand-over slice 3: the peer channel

- **Why.** D132, slice 3: one node talking to another it approves, with the checks of design 3.2, before any device is signed in. Nothing is deployed and no device is carried yet.
- **What.** `pvj/peer.py`: a call tries every address held for the node id (private addresses only) and uses the first whose certificate chains to the pinned root, covers the address, has the key the list or acceptance gives for that id, and (only when the clock was set from the network) has not run out. Each call carries the caller's list serial and deny digest: a lower serial is refused, a higher one sends its list, which goes through `take_list` (floor and fleet pin included). One outbound call at a time per node id; failures, not attempts, count toward 10 a minute for the node and 5 for each address. `POST /api/fleet/peer` (third route in `policy.NO_GATE`, TLS only, no token) answers a hello; a caller that is not approved costs a lookup and a count. Removals spread by asking and only grow. `POST /api/fleet/accepted` now takes only the id, the carry and the typed fingerprint: the name and key come from the peer's card over the checked connection, so a lying card cannot confirm itself (the slice 3 trap). `accepted/look` and `sync` are new. A list on disk that could not be checked at start is read again once the root or the certificate appears. The status keeps a line while the previous list is in use (R1), and `fleet push` tells the owner to remove "boxcert push" under People if its logout fails (R2).
- **Found.** Nothing authenticates the caller of a hello, and an "Allow again" at one node is undone by the next hello from a node that still holds the deny. Both are in design section 18 for review and slice 4d.
- **Checked.** tests/test_peer.py runs two or three real nodes over local TLS servers with certificates from throwaway roots: good peer, another root, the right root and the wrong key, a name that does not cover the address, a non-private address, a node that hangs, two addresses with the first dead, a lower serial refused, a higher adopted after verification (and not when it fails the checks or is another fleet), a run-out certificate refused only with a trusted clock, one slot per node, failures counted and attempts not, unknown callers, denies spreading and an allow never taken, and accepting by a card. tests/test_handover_api.py runs the route on the real server, and the new routes are in the role and support tables.
