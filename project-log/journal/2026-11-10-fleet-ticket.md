## 2026-11-10, last residual of the review (PR 269)

- At A, refused redeems from an approved, authenticated peer are no longer counted at all (the 20 a minute per peer is gone): four addresses posting junk forms to B could still fill it and block every real hand-over. The event line stays; callers A does not approve still count against their own address. Test: junk forms through B over eight addresses, then a real hand-over works.

## 2026-11-10, review fixes for slice 4a (PR 269)

- M1: `NO_CHECK_TIME` was 0x200000000 (bit 33), which OpenSSL ignores; the real flag is 0x200000. The peer handshake therefore judged dates and a node with a wrong clock could not hand over. Fixed, and a test makes a handshake with an expired and a not-yet-valid certificate (signed with `openssl ca` dates): it passes while `clock_trusted()` is false and is refused afterwards when it is true. The 32-bit remark in design section 19.3 and the addendum is gone.
- M2: the throttles no longer have a count for the whole node. B counts attempts (6 forms a minute per address) before calling A and failures (5) per address; A counts a refusal to an approved peer against that peer alone (20 a minute) and anything else against its own address (5). The A side (redeem) and B side (signin, confirm) have separate counters. Tests: junk forms from one address, from forty addresses, and junk passed on to A do not stop a real hand-over from another device or B's role as a caller.
- L3: `confirm` asks again whether the node is approved (not denied, carry still above none) and caps the role again. L4: the switch is read again under the settings lock at the moment the record is written (`Auth.add_node_device(allowed=)`), so a switch-off during an in-flight call is not undone. L5: `authenticate` also refuses a certificate that is not yet valid, while the clock is trusted. L6: `authenticate` catches the errors an odd certificate can raise (overflow, decoding, type) and answers 403.
- To remember: carried records have no expiry until 4b; the switch signs out `via: node` devices but not the Owners, codes or PIN pairings a carried Owner made while it was in.

## 2026-11-10, node handover slice 4a: the one-time ticket and the peer listener (D132)

- Built `pvj/ticket.py`. Node A keeps a ticket (128 random bits, as a hash, in memory, 64 at most, 60 seconds on the monotonic clock, six a minute per device) for one device and one node it has just checked. The device's browser posts it to node B (`POST /handover`, one urlencoded form of 2 KB at most); B calls A back once over the peer port (`POST /api/handover/redeem`) and A answers once: the look-up and the delete are one step under A's lock, so a ticket is spent whether or not the rest passes. B then makes an ordinary device record of its own (`via: node`, `secure`, `from {node, device}`, name "device, via node-01", at most 8 per source node, counted in the shared pool beside guests) with the lowest of what A says the device is now and B's carry for A. The same device again gets the same record and a new token. A page answers with `Set-Cookie: __Host-pvj_token` and a file of the panel's own (`/handover.js`) that moves to the panel; a valid cookie of a higher role is never replaced without a Switch button (the pending hand-over is in memory only, 120 seconds, 8 at most).
- Built the peer listener: `PeerServer` on `PVJ_PEER_PORT` (8443 in the unit), TLS with `CERT_REQUIRED` and the pinned root as the only CA (`HttpsBox.peer_context`), answering `/api/fleet/peer` and `/api/handover/redeem` only. The caller is proved by the handshake: `Peers.authenticate` reads clientAuth, the dates (only while the clock is trusted) and the key hash from the certificate it showed, and `handle_peer` and `redeem` use that key, never the one in the body. The hello of slice 3 moved here from the panel's port, which no longer answers a peer call. This closes the open item of slice 3 (nothing authenticated the caller of a hello).
- Rules held: only approved nodes count (list or fingerprint, and a deny made even after the ticket wins); a carried role is never raised (Owner with carry live gives Operator, Operator with carry full stays Operator, none gives nothing, a Guest, a code, a controller, a device paired over plain http, a support login and a device that came from another node never carry); the switch "Accept devices from other nodes" is the lock, Owner only (a carried Owner too), and turning it off now signs out every carried device. Left for 4b to 4d: lease, poke, the 24 hour lapse, the route-table walk, removal of a node.
- Tests: `tests/test_ticket.py` (two real nodes, a fake clock: one-time use also under eight threads, expiry at 60 seconds, replay, wrong node, wrong secret, wrong key in the body, denied node before and after the ticket, node not approved, carry none, every row of the role table 4a can reach, device removed or demoted between ticket and redeem, same device again, the ninth device, Origin, the throttles at both ends, the confirm, no retry, no secret in any log, event or answer) and `tests/test_ticket_http.py` (the real server: the form, the cookie, the confirm page, plain http, the sweep, who changes the switch, the ticket route, the redeem route on the real listener). The listener rejecting a caller without a valid client certificate (none, another root, serverAuth only, a valid node claiming another node's key) is in `tests/test_handover_api.py`. `tests/peerfix.py` now runs the REAL peer listener for every test node.
- Not done, on purpose: the panel (slice 5), the `form-action` of the page's policy, announcing the peer port by mDNS (a node uses 8443 or the port in its discovery row). Nothing was deployed or run on a node. A node certificate signed before slice 3 has serverAuth only and is refused by the listener until it is signed again (same key, so the list stays valid).
