## 2026-11-10, node handover slice 4c: what a carried device can and cannot do, route by route (D132)

- Added `tests/test_carried_roles.py`, the inverse of `tests/test_roles.py`. It makes devices the way a hand-over does (`Auth.add_node_device`: an Owner, an Operator, and an Owner whose node gave only `live`, which is what `carried_role("full", "live")` returns) and walks every route in the table and in `policy.OUTSIDE` as each of them, beside the local Owner and Operator. A carried device gets exactly what a local device of its role gets, on all 186 routes; never a route above its role; and `handover.carried_role` never gives a higher role than either the device has at home or the node gives (a table over every pair of home role and carry, bad values included; none and Guest make no record).
- A carried Owner passes every route a local Owner passes (that is the owner's choice, design section 4), and a few of the routes that make permanent access are run for real on the secure listener beside a local Owner: the PIN, invites, a join code, recovery codes, the switch, and removing a local Owner. A carried Operator is held to the local Operator's answers on the same routes (no PIN, no Operator invite, no removing an Owner, never sees an Owner in People).
- The Owner's switch and everything about trusting a node stay the Owner's for a carried Operator and for a capped Owner. They are held by name, so the removal of a node (4d) cannot be added as an Operator's route: every route with fleet, cluster or deny in its path, and every POST under `/api/nodes`, must have the Owner as its minimum. Nothing under `/api/handover` may be added without the test noticing; the ticket is the only one.
- No chaining, on the real server: a carried Owner and a carried Operator both get 403 on `POST /api/handover/ticket`, for a third node and for the node they came from, no ticket is made, and each refusal is an event line. Asking the check directly shows a request cannot say the device has no `via`.
- A new route fails the test until `CARRIED` names it (SAME, or the one that is decided otherwise). Checked by adding a route to the table: the decisions test and the by-name test both fail.
- The walk found no route that was wrong: no code in the server was changed. The only route where a carried device differs from a local one is the ticket, and that is already refused in `Tickets.may_carry`; it now has a test through the route.
- Left for 4d and 5: removing a node from the cluster and the deny epochs, the panel. Not deployed.
