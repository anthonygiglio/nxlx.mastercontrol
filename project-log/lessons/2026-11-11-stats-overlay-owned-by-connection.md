## An mpv osd-overlay belongs to the connection that made it (2026-11-11)

The stats overlay (D133) passed its tests, was reviewed and was deployed, and drew nothing on a node running classic mpv: the panel said it was on and the screen showed no text. mpv ties an `osd-overlay` to the client that sent it and removes it when that client's connection closes, and every request of `pvj.player.Ipc` is a connection of its own, so the text was drawn and taken off again within the same request. pvj-render did not show it because it forwards the command to a connection it keeps itself. The fake player in the tests kept the overlay whatever happened to the connection, and mpv with no video output cannot show it, so nothing in the tests could see it. The cause was found on node-02 by taking screenshots through the player's own socket (`screenshot-to-file ... window`, which does include OSD text) one connection at a time: an overlay sent on a connection that stayed open was in the picture, and gone when that connection closed. Not the OSD level (it was tried at 0 to 3).

What we do now: anything mpv ties to a client (overlays, and in general script-like state) is sent through a connection kept open for as long as it should last (`Ipc.hold`, `HeldIpc`), and the test double models mpv's rule so a one-request connection loses the overlay. A feature that draws on the screen is looked at on the screen, on classic mpv and on pvj-render, before it is called done. Screenshots through the player socket are a quick way to do that without a capture card.
