## D132. A device paired on one node opens the others through a one-time ticket that the first node keeps and the second redeems (2026-11-09, proposed, not decided)

Proposed, waiting for Anthony. The owner's words (2026-11-09): once one node has paired a user's device, and the user may see other nodes, they should be able to open those nodes without pairing with each one. Design: `design/node-handover_2026-11-09.md`. Nothing is built.

What is proposed: node A makes a 128-bit ticket (60 seconds, one use, held in memory on A's monotonic clock, bound to node B and to a secret only the opening page holds). The page posts it to B as a form. B calls A back over a TLS connection checked against the pinned root and the fleet list, A answers once, and B signs the device in as an ordinary device of its own (`via: node`) with the same role or lower, never higher. Only Owners and Operators paired over https carry; guests, presenter codes, controller codes and remote support never do. An Owner carries only between nodes named in a fleet list that the owner signs with the root on his computer (step 2c, signed enrollment of nodes); without it Operator at most, and only where the node's Owner switched on Accept devices from other nodes (off by default). A hand-over device is checked against A every 10 minutes while in use, is removed when A removes it, and lapses 7 days after A last answered. Chosen over a ticket signed by A (Python cannot check signatures without openssl, and a Pi with no battery clock cannot agree on time) and over a shared device list (the browser would still have to hand its token to B, and it is step 3 in disguise).

Why: it is the smallest change that keeps the root key off the nodes, uses no wall clock, makes revocation live, and falls back to today's PIN screen whenever anything is wrong.

Cost / rules out: B must reach A while the browser waits (as discovery already needs); the page's content security policy gains `form-action` for the nodes it may open; a new event kind; about 300 lines in `pvj/handover.py`. Rules out hand-over for guests, controllers and remote support, hand-over over http, trust on first use of a changed key, and any sync of devices or settings between nodes. Four open questions are in section 12 of the design.
