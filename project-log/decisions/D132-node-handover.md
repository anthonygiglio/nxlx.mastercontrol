## D132. A device paired on one node opens the others through a one-time ticket that the first node keeps and the second redeems (2026-11-09, proposed, not decided)

Proposed, waiting for Anthony. The owner's words (2026-11-09): once one node has paired a user's device, and the user may see other nodes, they should be able to open those nodes without pairing with each one. Design: `design/node-handover_2026-11-09.md`. Nothing is built.

What is proposed: node A makes a 128-bit ticket (60 seconds, one use, held in memory on A's monotonic clock, bound to node B and to a secret only the opening page holds). The page posts it to B as a form whose Origin must be A's. B calls A back over TLS, A looks up and deletes the ticket in one step and answers once, and B signs the device in as a device of its own (`via: node`) with the same role or lower, never higher. Only Owners and Operators paired over https carry; guests, presenter codes, controller codes and remote support never do. **The root alone carries nothing**, since a stolen, lent or retired node still holds a key and certificate. Nodes trust each other only through a fleet list that the owner signs with the root on his computer (it sets each node's carry: full, live or none; step 2c), or, if the owner chooses, a one-time acceptance of another node by its key fingerprint. Every peer check binds the node id to a key hash and checks that the certificate covers the address. **A `via: node` device may not create, reveal, rotate or remove any credential at B** (PIN, codes, guest links, other devices, root, certificate, list, switches, factory reset); one rule in the gate and a test that walks every route. A hand-over device is checked against A every 10 minutes while in use, is removed when A removes it, and lapses 7 days after A last answered. A node leaves the fleet by a new signed list (a runbook in the design). Chosen over a ticket signed by A (no signature checking in Python, no shared clock on a Pi) and over a shared device list (the browser would still have to hand its token to B; it is step 3 in disguise).

Why: it is the smallest change that keeps the root key off the nodes, uses no wall clock, makes revocation live, and falls back to today's PIN screen whenever anything is wrong.

Cost / rules out: B must reach A while the browser waits (as discovery already needs); the page's content security policy gains `form-action` for the nodes it may open; a new event kind; the list stored beside tls/ and kept out of export and diagnostics; about 300 lines in `pvj/handover.py`. Rules out hand-over for guests, controllers and remote support, hand-over over http, trust on first use of a changed key, and any sync of devices or settings between nodes. Six open questions are in section 13 of the design.
