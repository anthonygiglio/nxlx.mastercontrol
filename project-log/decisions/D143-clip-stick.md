## D143. The node's own clip stick: an exFAT Nxx-CLIPS partition claimed by root with a secret, checked, and only its clips folder writable (2026-11-17)

A node may write to exactly one place on any USB stick: the clips folder of its own clip stick. Every other stick, and the rest of the clip stick, stays read-only (the mounter, `mctl/usb.py`). The plan is nxlx_shared/specs/usb-sticks-plan_2026-11-15.md (board card J-162). Changed after the independent review of 47ebe89 (below).

- **What makes a stick the node's.** All of: an exFAT partition labelled `Nxx-CLIPS` (two digits, `N01-CLIPS`); a claim record on the node, `/var/lib/nxlx/clip-stick.json`, a regular file of root's with mode 0600 (no link; anything else counts as no record): `{"label", "uuid", "token", "claimed"}`; the same label and, when the claim saw one, the same exFAT volume serial (blkid's UUID) on the partition; and the marker `nxlx-clip-stick.json` at the top of the partition (at most 4 KB, read without following a link): `{"nxlx_clip_stick": 1, "node_id": "<the fleet id>", "node_name": "nxlx-node-01", "label": "N01-CLIPS", "token": "<32 hex>", "claimed": "<UTC>"}` whose node id is this node's and whose token equals the record's (`hmac.compare_digest`). The token is `secrets.token_hex(16)`, new at every claim. The node id alone proves nothing: the fleet announcement (D127) gives it to the whole network. The USB serial is never looked at (both Verbatim sticks report 0000000005). No record means no clip stick, so a node that never claimed one mounts every stick as before.
- **Where the secret goes.** On the stick and in the root-only record, nowhere else: not in the status file, the public label file, the log, `/api/media` or the fleet. The panel learns only the label, from `/var/lib/nxlx/clip-stick-label.json` (0644, label and time), or that some stick is claimed (the record exists).
- **How a stick is claimed.** Root, from a terminal: `sudo nxlx-usb claim /dev/sdX2 [--force]` (the installer prints the line). It refuses a partition that is not exFAT Nxx-CLIPS, a node without an id, a stick that fails `fsck.exfat -p`, and a stick whose marker names another node unless `--force`. Under the lock it unmounts the partition if mounted, checks it, mounts it writable at a folder of its own under `/run/nxlx-usb/peek/` (0700), writes the marker with the new token (hidden temporary name, fsync, rename, fsync of the folder), makes `clips/`, unmounts (a failed unmount stops the claim, with no record), writes the record and the label file, and mounts the stick as below. `sudo nxlx-usb forget-clip-stick` removes both files. No panel action.
- **Mounting.** A lock (`flock` on `/run/nxlx-usb/clip.lock`, at most 15 minutes of waiting) is held by a claim and by the mount of any exFAT Nxx-CLIPS partition; other sticks never wait for it. With a record for this label and volume, the partition is mounted read-only at a folder of this call's own under `peek/` and its marker read; an unmount of that look that fails (it is then detached) or a mount or unmount that times out ends the look, and the stick is mounted as any other stick. With a matching marker: the status says `checking`, then `fsck.exfat -p` (exit 0 or 1 passes; at most 10 minutes; a time-out is logged and counts as a failure), then the whole partition is mounted at `/run/nxlx-usb/mnt/<dev>` (the folder is 0700: only root reaches the top of the stick) with `rw,nosuid,nodev,noexec,noatime,uid=0,umask=0002,gid=<nxlx-svc>,errors=remount-ro`, tried once without `errors=` if the driver refuses it (as far as the kernel's exfat source is known here, it takes it; not checked on the nodes' kernels). The marker is read again on that mount; if it no longer matches (the stick was swapped), it is unmounted, no status is written, and the stick is mounted as any other. Then `clips/` (made when writable and missing) is bound at `/media/nxlx-clips` and that binding remounted `bind,rw|ro,nosuid,nodev,noexec`. A failed or missing check mounts everything read-only, the binding too, even with `NXLX_USB_RW=1`. A read-only stick without `clips/` stays mounted privately and says so. The status file `/run/nxlx-usb/clip-stick.json` (0644) holds the device, the label, the state (`checking`, `in`, `read-only`, `no-clips`) and why; it names no path. Unmount takes the binding off first, then the private mount, and removes the status first of all.
- **Why only the clips folder.** exFAT has no permissions per file or folder: uid, gid and umask belong to the whole filesystem, and a bind mount shares them. What a bind mount does have of its own is read-only and nosuid, nodev, noexec. So the top of the stick (the marker) is only under a folder no service can enter, and the one writable place is the binding of `clips/`. nxlx-web gets `ReadWritePaths=-/media/nxlx-clips` (only that folder; a binding already there when the service starts would be read-only in its namespace without it). The top of the clip stick is under no drive folder, so the recovery stick scan (D81), the USB drive list and `/media/usb` never see it; a stick that is not the node's (a forged marker included) is any other stick, with D81 as before.
- **The media folder.** `Api.media_dir` is asked each time (cached for one second): `/media/nxlx-clips` while the status says `in` or `read-only` and that folder is a real mount point (no link), else the folder `NXLX_MEDIA_DIR` names (the SD card's). Each operation reads the folder and the state once, together (`Api._media_now`): the media list, upload and copy (free space, the sweep of leftovers), delete and rename. `/api/media` carries `clip_stick`: `none` (nothing claimed, nothing said: node-02), `in`, `checking`, `read-only`, `no-clips` or `missing`, each but the first two with a plain sentence the panel shows as one line at the top of the library. While a claimed stick is missing, being checked, read-only or without clips, an upload or a copy is refused with a plain sentence ("The clip stick is not plugged in, so nothing was stored. Plug it in and try again.") instead of landing on the SD card.
- **Writes.** Uploads and copies are a hidden `.upload-` file, fsync, then a hard link (no overwrite) or a rename, then an fsync of the folder. exFAT has no hard links and refuses chmod to a mode its mask does not give (both EPERM): on that error alone the file is renamed after checking, under the media lock every write in the panel holds, that the name is still free, and the chmod is passed over. Rename of a clip takes the same road and fsyncs the folder.
- **Factory reset.** The media folder is resolved once, checked, and that same folder is the one emptied. A node with a claimed clip stick never deletes clips in a reset, even while the stick reads missing.
- **Never a recovery file.** Nothing writes `nxlx-recover` (D81) on any stick; a test holds that a claimed stick has only the marker and `clips`.

Why: the clips must outlive an SD card and move between computers (exFAT is the one format macOS, Windows and Linux all read and write with files over 4 GB), and a node writing to a stick that is not its own, or to one with a damaged table, is how a show's clips are lost. The review of 47ebe89 found that a marker naming the public node id could be forged, that the whole stick was writable, and that a reset could check one folder and empty another; those are closed here. Cost / rules out: a stick is the node's only after a root command, and again after a new SD card or a lost record; a stick re-formatted or copied to another stick needs a new claim (the volume serial); a stick that fails its check is read-only until repaired on a computer; only one clip stick per node (one fixed binding); clips at the top of the clip stick are not listed (they belong in `clips/`); exFAT has no journal, so a pull during a write can still lose that file; the check delays a large stick's mount at boot; while a claimed stick is out, nothing can be uploaded. Not proven: nothing here has run on a node or met a real stick; the bind and remount commands, the mount options, `errors=remount-ro`, the fsck exit codes, the namespace of nxlx-web at boot and the EPERM behaviour of exFAT are from documentation and source, tested only with stand-ins.
