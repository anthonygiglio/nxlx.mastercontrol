## D137. On a PC node, Automatic sound is the HDMI or DisplayPort sound of the screen the picture is on, matched by ELD, then by port order (2026-11-14)

What: on any node that is not a Pi, the sound output Automatic (`/api/audio`, `device: auto`) means the HDMI or DisplayPort sound device of the connector the picture is on, when it can be told, and mpv's own choice when it cannot. The connector is the one the player said it draws on (the screen report, D128), else the chosen output when connected, else the first connected HDMI screen, then DisplayPort.

The sound is read by port, not by ELD entry. The HDA codec lists ELD files `/proc/asound/cardN/eld#CODEC.N`; with DP MST support each port has several entries, N = port times streams plus stream. The streams a port come from the codec file's "Devices: S" lines (`/proc/asound/cardN/codec#CODEC`), else from the entries over the HDMI PCMs, else 1. An Intel Kaby Lake HD 630 has 3 ports, 9 entries (eld#0.0 to eld#0.8, its monitors on 0, 3 and 6) and 3 PCMs. A port has a monitor when any of its entries has one. Port k's PCM device is the one the driver names `HDMI k` (`pcmDp/info`; 3, 7 and 8 on Intel), else HDA's fixed 3, 7, 8, 9 ...

A connector is matched to a port in two ways, in this order:

1. **By monitor name**, when no other connected screen has that name and exactly one port with a monitor has it: the EDID in `/sys/class/drm/cardN-NAME/edid` and the port's ELD carry the same name.
2. **By port order**, when names cannot tell (no name, or two screens giving one name, as two capture cards of one model do): the connected screens, all HDMI or all DisplayPort, in the kernel's order (HDMI-A-1, -2, -3), are matched with the ports that have a monitor, in order, when there are as many of each. **This is an assumption:** that the kernel numbers its connectors in the order of the sound ports. It held on the Kaby Lake M710q, where HDMI-A-1 took PCM 3. A port whose ELD names another monitor than the connector's EDID is never taken.

The mpv name used is `alsa/plughw:CARD=X,DEV=D` (the one measured), else `alsa/hdmi:CARD=X,DEV=k`, and only when mpv lists it. The periodic check (`ensure_audio`) and the player's restart after an output change (`apply_audio`) follow the picture. An output the Owner chose by hand is kept as it is. A Pi is unchanged: its vc4hdmi rule comes first, and the PC rule never runs on a Pi.

Why: on a PC the ALSA default is the analog jack, so Automatic sent the sound away from the screen. Measured on an Intel M710q (Debian 13, HD 630): a test tone on Automatic never reached the HDMI capture card on HDMI-A-1; on `alsa/plughw:CARD=PCH,DEV=3` it did, at -24 dB. The first version counted ELD entries as ports, and on the real node (9 entries, monitors on 0, 3 and 6) only the first screen got a device; reading the ports fixes that.

Cost / rules out: the port order assumption may not hold on another board, where the kernel's connector numbers and the codec's ports run in a different order; there the sound could go to another screen of the same model, and the Owner chooses by hand. An HDMI and a DisplayPort screen together, with names that cannot tell, are not matched. Two monitors on one DP MST port are not told apart (the port's first is taken). On newer Intel graphics the driver may give a port the first free PCM instead of its own (dynamic PCM assignment); the `HDMI k` order is assumed. AMD and NVIDIA HDMI sound use the same ELD files and are expected to work the same way, unmeasured. The panel needs no change: it already shows `automatic_is`.
