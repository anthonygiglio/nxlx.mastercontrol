## D117. The box follows an Ableton Link session through a small helper, and only follows (2026-10-31)

A new C++ program, `pvj-link` (`render/link/`, GPL-3.0-or-later because Link is GPLv2+), built at install from Debian's `ableton-link-dev` and never vendored, runs as its own service with its own account and group, no capabilities, a read-only system, no devices and only `AF_INET`, `AF_INET6`, `AF_NETLINK` and `AF_UNIX`. Off by default: only the Owner switches it on (`POST /api/tempo/link`, the setting `link`), and a support session may switch it off, never on. It joins a session, reads the tempo and the place in a bar of 4, and writes them to a small file at most 20 times a second; the panel reads that file where it reads the levels and gives it to the box clock's `link` slot, which D116 put above the hand. The clock has a new word, `Clock.link(bpm, phase, at)`: the place in the bar at a named time. It sets the phase at once, the shortest way round, and keeps the clock's own beat count. Every other D116 rule stands; a word's age is counted from the record's own time, so a helper that stopped goes quiet at the right moment.

**While alone, the Link object stays disabled and the helper only listens** for another peer's "alive" message on Link's multicast address, sending nothing. It enables Link 0.75 s after the first one it hears, and disables it again a second after the last peer goes. This goes beyond the brief, which asked for Link to be enabled while the switch is on, because that would have broken the brief's first rule. When two Link sessions meet the older one wins and the younger takes its tempo (`ableton/link/Sessions.hpp`, a margin of 0.5 s). A box left on alone would own the older session, and the next program to switch Link on would be moved to Link's default of 120 BPM: seen on a desk with two plain peers, the newer one went from 133 to 120. With the wait the box is always the younger session. It also means that on and alone the box says nothing on the network.

A record says "this was the place in the bar at this time" on CLOCK_MONOTONIC. Link's clock on Linux is CLOCK_MONOTONIC_RAW, not the box's, so the two are read side by side (the brief asked to check; the shared clock it hoped for is not there). A record reaches the clock only from a helper that is on, with at least 1 peer, no older than 0.5 s. With no peers the file has no tempo at all, so Link's default can reach nothing.

The setting is a section of its own, `link`, in place of a key in `sound`: `sound`'s form refuses unknown keys, so a rollback to an earlier release would have read every sound section as damaged, and off. It is the box's own (`boxcare.BOX_OWN`), and the panel joins the group `pvj-link` to reach the helper's folder, so the panel's next start is what makes it reachable.

Why a helper: pvj-render is C and is not touched, the panel stays Apache-2.0 by talking to a GPL program over a socket as it does with pvj-render, and the one part that takes packets from anyone on the network stays apart from everything else. Why not a beat tracker or MIDI clock: D116; MIDI clock in still waits for a device to test with.

Cost / rules out: it does not lead a session and cannot: no tempo, no beat, no play and stop go out, and it cannot be the first in a session. Two nodes with nobody else never start one, so Link cannot sync nodes to each other on its own; use the sync of `sync/` for that. It joins about 0.8 s later than a plain Link program. Link has no authentication: anyone on the network who joins moves the tempo the box follows, which is why it is off by default and why Link outranking the hand matters: a person tapping while Link steers does nothing until Link goes quiet (D116's choice). A session tempo outside 30 to 300 BPM is refused by the clock, so the box holds its last tempo through it. Only IPv4 is listened to before joining; a network with IPv6 only would never wake it. Once enabled, Link uses every running network, as Link does; the support tunnel has no multicast. Known narrow gap: two boxes that both join a peer which vanishes inside that same second, before they meet it, could pair with each other at whatever tempo Link last held (120 on a fresh start), and with a peer each they would stay there until one is switched off. Two boxes left in a session after the program quits likewise hold its last tempo. It adds 3 packages and a compile of about 485 MB of memory at its peak (9 s on a desk, unmeasured on a Pi; the image's build under emulation may hit the 20 minute limit, in which case that image has no Link until its next install). There is no new page: one switch and one line on the Sound page, until the owner reworks the UI. Not run: a Pi, Ableton Live, a phone, a real network, the sandbox of the unit, the installer for real. `tools/DEVICE-TESTING.md` LK1 to LK7 are for that.
