## Addendum to D106 (2026-10-30): the render thread runs at real-time priority 1, and the final pass clears first; hardware decoding is not adopted

**What the playback measurements of 2026-10-30 found** (a scratch pvj-render on the test node, a Pi 4, 4 sources at 720p, mapping on, 1080p output, 20 s windows; `measurements/2026-10-30-playback`). The flips fell to 40.7 a second for two reasons: the GPU (the mapping's final pass costs 14.6 ms a flip, with 5 texture reads for each of 2.07 million pixels) and the CPU (the four decoders take about 260 percent of a core, and the render thread waited for one: its worst stage was 82 to 91 ms while it was only 40 to 44 percent busy).

**Done now (fix 1 of the five in the README).**
- The render thread asks for SCHED_RR at priority 1, once, before its loop, and says in the log whether it got it. Only that thread: the call is made on the calling thread, and SCHED_RESET_ON_FORK keeps any thread or process it starts later at normal priority. The decoders (libmpv's threads, made earlier or by libmpv's own thread) are never real-time. If the call is refused (no right, a desktop, CI, an older unit) the program says so once and runs as before; it never fails to start for this.
- The final pass clears its target to black before drawing. Both final passes, the plain scale and the mapping's shader, draw a quad over the whole target, with blending, scissor and the colour mask reset, so the clear changes no pixel; it lets the GPU skip loading the old frame. Measured in the scratch build: 40.7 to 44.9 flips a second, and with the real-time thread 45.3 and no flip longer than 2 vblanks.
- The player unit gets `LimitRTPRIO=1`, the narrowest way: a resource limit that lets an unprivileged account ask for SCHED_RR 1, no capability. The unit has no CapabilityBoundingSet, NoNewPrivileges, RestrictRealtime or SystemCallFilter, so nothing else had to change (the NDI helper's unit sets RestrictRealtime=yes and stays as it is). The limit covers the whole unit, classic mpv included, but it grants no priority by itself and mpv never asks; classic gains no right it uses.
- **Why a runaway thread cannot lock out the panel.** Priority 1 is the lowest real-time level, but any real-time thread outranks every normal one, so what protects the panel is the kernel's real-time throttle: `sched_rt_runtime_us` 950000 in a `sched_rt_period_us` of 1000000, so real-time threads together get at most 95 percent of a second. Also the loop is one thread, which can hold one core of the four. Measured on a WSL kernel (6.18), not on the Pi: one runaway RR-1 thread on 4 cores cost a normal task nothing (worst loop 12 ms); four of them on 4 cores left it running at 301 loops against 518, with stalls of up to 961 ms (the 5 percent gap comes at the end of each second). The Pi's kernel setting was not read; it is one `cat /proc/sys/kernel/sched_rt_runtime_us` in the node check (DEVICE-TESTING, RT1). If it reads -1 the throttle is off and one runaway thread could hold a core.

**Hardware decoding: do not adopt v4l2m2m-copy (D105 gate 4).** On the same node CPU fell by more than half but the flips fell too (4 sources, mapping off: 53.2 software, 35.1 with v4l2m2m-copy; mapping on: 39.8 and 29.2), because the render stage blocks; sources 3 and 4 logged "could not consume packet" and one reported no hwdec. Four clips at 1080p wedged the firmware codec (`failed to create component ril.video_decode` until a reboot). pvj-render as shipped uses `hwdec no` and is not affected. The zero-copy paths (v4l2m2m, drm) were not measured: they were run after the wedge and fell back to software without saying so. Gate 4 is met in software: 4 sources at 720p, 2 at 1080p, clips guided to 720p at exactly 30 fps.

**Not done here:** fixes 2 to 4 (the display scaler at 720 lines, the lock to every second vblank, the loop seam). The numbers above are from 20 s windows with a scratch build; this build has not run on the node.
