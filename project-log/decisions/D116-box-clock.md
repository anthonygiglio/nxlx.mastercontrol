## D116. One box clock: a flywheel fed by tap, OSC and sound, not a new beat tracker (2026-10-31)

The box now has one clock (`pvj/clock.py`). Once it has a tempo it keeps a tempo and a beat position by itself, through gaps in whatever feeds it. Its sources, most trusted first, are `link` (a slot only: nothing feeds it yet), `tap` (taps, or a tempo typed in), `osc` (the existing `/nxlx/sound/in/beat` and `/bpm`) and `sound` (pvj-sound's tempo and beats). A source that goes quiet hands over to the next; when all are quiet the last good tempo keeps running and the status says which source steered it last and how old its word is. Sound is believed only at a confidence of 0.5 or more, and then only to set the tempo and to push the phase gently (a hit further than 0.3 beat from the grid is ignored, a nearer one moves the phase by 15 percent of its distance), so a stray hit cannot jump the beat. A tap or an OSC beat sets the phase at once. Taps are averaged (2 to 8, a pause over 2 seconds starts again, 30 to 300 BPM or refused). The clock has no thread: the panel's once-a-second tick moves it on, and whoever asks for a value does too.

Consumer: while a tap or OSC steers the clock, the levels record that the panel's meter, OSC out (`/nxlx/sound/pulse`, `bpm`, `beat`) and the classic renderer's shader input read has the clock's pulse, tempo and beat count in place of sound's, with or without a sound device (the Owner's sound switch is still the Owner's; with no device the levels are zero and nothing is heard). With no tap or OSC clock set, sound's values are exactly what they were. The levels file and pvj-render are not changed. The tap is one action, `clock_tap`, for MIDI, OSC (`/pvj/clock/tap`) and DMX (an optional tenth channel). The routes are `GET /api/clock`, `POST /api/clock` and `POST /api/clock/tap`; a support session may look and may not change.

Why: the owner wants the under-the-hood work wrapped up so he can rework the UI next, and the beat was the one thing that stopped between onsets. **Why not a new beat tracker:** the node's CPU is already near 80 percent, nobody has benchmarked the options on a Pi 4, and live tempo tracking is unreliable at best on real music. A flywheel that a person or a program can correct costs a few microseconds a call and fails in a way a person can fix with one tap. The user still chooses the audio source for sound reactivity (h30): the clock listens to nothing of its own; OSC beats reach it only through the sound source "Levels sent in over OSC", from the addresses the Owner listed, as before.

**Next:** Ableton Link comes next, into the `link` slot (`Clock.hear("link", bpm)`). MIDI clock in waits for a device to test with. The tempo is not saved: it starts empty after a restart, because saving it would have meant widening the sound setting's strict form and its tests; one tap, or the first confident sound or OSC tempo, starts it.

Cost / rules out: a tap holds the clock for 30 seconds before a confident sound tempo takes over again (OSC and Link: 5 seconds), and the sound hand-over is felt as a tempo change, never a jump of position. A person tapping while Link is steering does nothing (Link is more trusted). OSC beats and tempos still need the Owner to choose "Levels sent in over OSC" as the sound source and to list the sender. The beat message over OSC counts from the clock's origin, not pvj-sound's own count. Nothing here has met a real tapper, a Max patch or a band; `tools/DEVICE-TESTING.md` BC1 to BC5 are not yet run on a node.
