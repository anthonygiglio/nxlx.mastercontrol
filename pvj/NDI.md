# NDI® input (beta; the picture has had one first run on a Pi 4; nobody has heard its sound yet)

Show the picture of Resolume, MadMapper, OBS or any other NDI sender on the box's screen, like a clip. **With its sound** since 2026-10-08, when the owner said "go ahead with sound": the sound a source sends is played with its picture through the box's own sound output, volume and mute, and one switch on the page turns it off. **Nobody has heard it yet** (see "Sound"). **It is opt-in per box:** a box that was only installed has nothing of NDI on it and nothing of it runs; one root command at the box sets it up (see "Setting NDI up on a box"). Then switch it on under System > NDI input. Information about NDI and its tools is at [ndi.video](https://ndi.video/); the free tools (Test Patterns, Screen Capture, Studio Monitor) are at [ndi.video/tools](https://ndi.video/tools/).

NDI® is a registered trademark of Vizrt NDI AB.

**State on 2026-10-08.** The receiver, its service, the routes and the page are written and tested against a fake library and fake frames. The library layer met the real NDI library on a Mac on 2026-10-07 (see "Measured against the real library"). **On 2026-10-08 it ran for the first time on a Pi 4** (the owner's test Pi, Debian 13, mpv 0.40): the opt-in command worked, the library loaded inside the sandbox, senders were found, and a moving source was received and played by mpv for a minute with no frame dropped by the helper (see "First run on a Pi 4"). **That was one short run, from a sender on Wi-Fi, by SSH**; the colours and geometry were judged from one preview picture, and a still source did not give a snapshot. The owner then looked at the moving source on the Pi's monitor and said "The pi looked good": that is the first look by eye, at a moving source only; the still source has not been looked at. **Sound was built after that run and has not been on the box: nobody has heard it.** Not measured at all: the sound on a box, lip sync, latency, 60 frames a second, 1080p60, NDI HX, an interlaced sender, a run of five minutes or more, and everything under "Not verified". **The licence:** the owner has decided to accept the NDI SDK licence himself, for testing on his own test Pi only. **Whether a venue box may use the library is still open, and is the owner's question to settle with NDI** (see "The SDK License Agreement itself").

## Sound (built 2026-10-08; measured on the dev Mac; nobody has heard it)

The owner said "go ahead with sound" on 2026-10-08. What is here was built and tested on the dev Mac, against fakes and against the real senders of NDI Tools through the real library, with nothing played aloud. **What a box plays, how late, whether lips and voice agree, and how loud it is in the room are device step N10.**

**What it does.** For the source on the screen the helper also takes the source's sound from the library, and the one mpv plays it with the picture on the output the box already uses, under the box's own volume, mute and choice of sound device (the Sound page). The helper opens no sound device and its sandbox is unchanged.

**How the sound travels.** With sound, the pipe no longer carries bare frames. It carries a stream the helper writes itself: Matroska with uncompressed video (the same UYVY bytes, FourCC `UYVY`) and 32-bit float PCM, with a time on every frame and on every block of sound. The stream has no length of its own (it ends when the pipe does); each frame and each block of sound is one cluster with its exact length. mpv reads it with its own Matroska reader, named in the options so that it does not guess, and for that one load the player is narrowed to the two decoders the stream needs (see "Safety"). A source that sends no sound, and every source when sound is switched off, is played exactly as before: bare frames and the raw video options, which is the path that ran on the Pi.

**Why a stream with times, and not a second pipe of raw sound.** A player given two raw pipes tells the time by counting: frame 300 is at ten seconds, sample 480000 is at ten seconds. This helper drops a frame whenever the screen is slow (the newest wins); each drop would move the picture by a whole frame against the sound, for good. So the helper says when each thing belongs, and the player keeps them together.

**Whose clock.** The helper's own, never the sender's. Measured against the real senders: the time the library gives a block of sound is when it was sent, and jitters (steps of 35 to 165 ms between blocks of exactly 100 ms), and Test Patterns' one still frame carried a time 8061 seconds older than its sound. So a frame belongs where it arrived, and sound is placed by counting its samples from where its first block arrived (one block's length earlier, since a block is sent when its last sample exists). The times in the pipe always follow one another by that count: there is never a step back or a hole in them for the player to make something of.

**How the count is kept at the clock** (changed after the review of 2026-10-08, which showed that the first build bounded the error at 0.3 s and never took it away: one hiccup left the sound up to 0.3 s late for good). A block cannot reach the helper before its last sample exists. So a single block that shows the count ahead of the clock by more than 0.04 s proves that it is (lateness on the way can only hide a lead, never make one), and exactly that much sound is cut from the front of the block, at once. The count behind the clock is believed only when every block of a whole second said so (one late block proves nothing); then that much silence is put in. More than 0.3 s behind is a gap (the sender stopped and is back): up to a second of silence leads up to the sound. Both ways the correction is real samples cut or real silence added, so the times stay one after another, and each is counted as "adjusted". 0.04 s is one picture at 25 a second and about where sound that comes before its picture starts to be noticed (as I remember ITU-R BT.1359: about 45 ms for sound that is early, 125 ms for sound that is late; not looked up again for this). The cost is that each correction can be heard as a tick: once after a hiccup, and with a sender whose clock differs from the box's by one part in ten thousand, once in about seven minutes. Stretching the sound to avoid that would need resampling in the helper, which was not built. Nobody has heard any of it (step N10).

**What happens when.**

- **No sound from the source:** after the first picture the helper waits half a second for sound; if none comes, the source is shown as bare frames. If sound starts later, the helper ends the stream as "changed" and the panel opens the source again, now with sound: the picture is gone for a second or two, once.
- **The rate or the number of channels changes:** the same "changed" and reopening, as when a picture changes size.
- **The sound stops while the picture goes on** (the sender mutes, or blocks are lost): the helper writes silence for the stretch that is missing, so the player does not hold the picture back waiting for sound. Counted as "filled". Sound in the pipe trails the picture by a block's length as a matter of course, so silence is written only once two blocks' lengths are missing (never sooner than 0.3 s), and only up to a block's length before the picture, where the next real block will begin. The silence's place is taken (the count moved past it) before a byte of it is written, in the same step in which the waiting sound was taken, so nothing that is queued meanwhile can be given a time inside it. Sound that was only late and comes after its silence is cut by the rule above, so it does not stay late.
- **The screen is slow or the player stops reading:** at most one second of sound, and never more than 256 blocks, waits in the queue; beyond that the oldest is dropped and counted. That is the queue's bound: the thread that writes may also hold the one batch it had already taken from the queue (again a second at most) while the pipe is full. It never piles up beyond that.
- **A still picture with sound** (Test Patterns): the one frame, then only sound; each block follows the last by count.
- **More than two channels:** the first two are played. NDI says how many channels there are and nothing about what each one is, so there is nothing to mix down by. (The Mac's "macOS AV Output" sender sent six, with sound only in the first two.) A mono source is played as one channel.
- **Rates:** 32, 44.1, 48, 88.2 and 96 kHz are taken as they are (mpv converts for the output). Anything else is not played, and the page says so; the picture goes on.
- **Blocks of sound longer than a quarter of a second** are not played: the block is refused and counted, the page says "the source sends its sound in blocks of 0.60 seconds; blocks longer than 0.25 are not played", and the picture goes on (as bare frames, if no shorter block ever comes). Why a bound at all: a block reaches the helper when its last sample exists, so the sound in the pipe trails the picture by a block's length, and the player has to hold that much uncompressed picture while it waits (4 MB a frame at 1080p). With no bound, the review showed, blocks of 0.6 s and longer were played as silence for ever without a word (the silence written for the "missing" sound always got there first). Why a quarter of a second: the real senders measured on 2026-10-08 sent 0.1 s (Test Patterns) and 0.017 s (macOS AV Output), and this is two and a half times the longest; no sender with longer blocks is known here. If one turns up, the constant is `MAX_AUDIO_BLOCK` in `pvj/ndi.py`, and the player's queue (below) follows from it.

**The player's queue** (`Player.pipe_queue`). mpv's limit on what it holds of a stream that it reads (`demuxer-max-bytes`) is one total for the picture's and the sound's packets together. Read in `demux/demux.c` at v0.35.0, v0.37.0 and v0.40.0, the same in all three: when the total reaches the limit while one of the two has nothing waiting, mpv writes "Too many packets in the demuxer packet queues" once and plays on as if that one had ended for the moment, which for this stream would be the sound cutting out. The first build gave the stream the 32 MiB that bare frames get, which is eight frames at 1080p, less than the picture that comes in while a 0.25 s block of sound is on its way at 60 frames a second. Now the limit is one second of picture at the stream's size and rate, never under 32 MiB and never over 256 MiB: 53 MiB at 720p30, 119 MiB at 1080p30, 238 MiB at 1080p60. One second covers the longest block (0.25 s), the sound the player keeps ahead for its device (0.2 s by default) and the helper's own lag, with room. It is a ceiling, not what is used: in step, the player holds about a block's length of picture. **Not measured on a box**; step N10 says which line to watch.

**How loud.** NDI's sound is floating point in which 1.0 is a professional reference level (+4 dBu), not full scale; a sender may go well above 1.0. The SDK's header for its own 16-bit helpers describes a "reference level" in decibels for this and advises 20 when receiving, which puts full scale 20 dB above reference. The helper does the same, in one named constant (`AUDIO_HEADROOM_DB = 20` in `pvj/ndi.py`): every sample is multiplied by 0.1, and what would still be over full scale is held there. Measured: Test Patterns' tone is 1 kHz and peaks at exactly 1.0, so the pipe carries it peaking at -20.00 dBFS (RMS -23.01), which is where an SMPTE alignment tone belongs. **Not measured:** what level a program source such as a film through Scan Converter arrives at (Scan Converter sent no sound when it was tried). If such senders put digital full scale at 1.0 and not at 10.0, their sound will come out 20 dB quieter than a clip; step N10 compares the two by ear, and the constant is the one thing to change.

**What is refused.** Every figure of a block of sound comes from the network through closed code and is checked before a byte is read: the format (32-bit float planes only), the rate (from the list above), the channels (1 to 64 said, two read), the samples in a block (1 to 48000, and never more than a quarter of a second of them, see above), the distance between planes (at least a block, at most a bounded padding), and the address. A block that fails is counted and left out; it never ends the picture. A block that holds a NaN, an infinity or a sample more than 60 dB over reference is played as silence of the same length. The times the sender gives are not used at all.

**The switch.** Setup > NDI input, "Play a source's sound": OFF until someone switches it on (`"ndi": {"sound": true}` in the settings; no new schema, a file without the key means off). Off by default since 2026-10-11, the coordinator's call after the independent review: the sound is the one path never run on hardware, and the one that hands mpv's Matroska reader to whoever controls the helper; it stays off until it has been heard on a box. One edge of "no new schema": the build before sound refuses any key in that section but `addresses`, so if a box is ever taken back to it, a settings export made with the switch in it will not import there until the `sound` line is taken out by hand. The default lives in the panel, which tells the helper in so many words every time; the helper itself plays no sound unless told to. On by default because the owner asked for sound and a source's sound belongs with its picture as a clip's does; the box's volume and mute apply either way. Switching it off is also the way back to the picture path that was measured. Switching it while a source is on the screen reopens the source.

**What the page shows.** Whether sound is arriving, the channels and rate, the loudest sample of the last second in dB under full scale or "silent", and how many blocks were dropped, could not be read, or were played as silence.

**Measured on the dev Mac (2026-10-08), with the real library and NDI Tools, nothing played aloud.**

- **Test Patterns:** 48000 Hz, 2 channels, blocks of 4800 samples (100 ms), float planes, plane distance 19200 bytes. A 1 kHz tone (999.8 Hz by zero crossings), peak 1.00000, RMS 0.70709. Through the helper into a pipe for ten seconds: the stream was Matroska, decided 0.06 seconds after the first picture; 100 blocks received and 100 written, none dropped, each following the one before with no gap; the pipe's sound peaked at 0.10000, -20.00 dBFS. It was also written to a WAV outside the repository to look at; it was not played.
- **macOS AV Output:** 48000 Hz, 6 channels, blocks of 800 samples; sound in the first two channels only (peaks 0.33 and 0.35). It sent no picture when the helper was run against it, so it was not played through the pipe.
- **Scan Converter:** no sound in 8 seconds and none in a ten second run (shown as bare frames, decided after the half second's wait).
- **Cost:** converting ten seconds of 48 kHz stereo took 77 ms of one core on the Mac. A Pi 4 is several times slower; the figure there is not known.

**What CI shows, and what only the box can.** Where mpv is installed (CI's Linux runner; not the dev Mac) a test runs the helper's real writer into a real pipe read by the real mpv with the player's options, without a display or a sound device: mpv must take the stream from a pipe, find a 64 x 16 picture whose colour is the red that was sent (so the byte order survives the container), and write out 48 kHz stereo sound of the length that was sent with the tone at -20 dBFS. On 2026-10-08 that test ran on the runner's mpv 0.37 and passed at the first try, on Python 3.9 and 3.12. That shows mpv can read the stream. After the review two more tests were written for the real mpv, on a Mac that has none, so **their first run is CI's** (the journal says how it went): one gives mpv a stream of the same shape whose picture track says Motion JPEG and whose sound track says integer PCM, once without the narrowing (mpv must decode both, which shows the file is one mpv plays) and once with the options the player sets (mpv must decode neither and say "Failed to initialize a decoder" for each); the other sends 1280 x 720 at 30 a second with sound in quarter-second blocks, in real time, and mpv's log must not hold "Too many packets in the demuxer packet queues" with the queue the player gives, and must hold it with a queue of 2 MiB (which shows the run can see the line). The first test passes the options on mpv's command line, where the player sets the same names and values as properties: the same option parser, not the same call. None of it shows anything a person would hear, the delay, or whether sound and picture are in step on a real screen with real loudspeakers: mpv runs there as fast as it can, and the runner's mpv is not the Pi's (0.40) nor Bookworm's (0.35; its Matroska reader was read and takes the same elements, which is not the same as having been run).

## One sound source for the whole box (D87, 2026-10-12; tried by hand on the Pi's network, never run by a box, nobody has heard it)

The owner's rig: the picture comes from NDI Scan Converter (its size changes at every clip) and the sound from a separate sender that has sound and no picture (VLC playing into NDI's sound device). Such a sender cannot be played as a source: the helper waits for a first picture and answers "sent no picture".

**What is built (2026-10-12): the helper, the settings, the API, the page's card "Where sound comes from", the "sound only" label and the delay in the player. Tried by hand on the test Pi's network with this code; never installed on a box; the page was checked for syntax only.**

**What it does.** The Owner names one of the allowed sources as where the sound comes from. Its sound is then played under whatever NDI picture is on the screen. Three states of two settings: sound off (`"sound": false`); each picture's own sound (`"sound": true` and no `sound_from`); one source for every picture (`"sound": true, "sound_from": "<id>"`). Sound off keeps the choice.

**How.** The sound has a connection of its own, which belongs to the helper's service and not to the picture (`SoundFeed`). It is opened for sound alone: `NDIlib_recv_bandwidth_audio_only`, 10 (see "Checked" below). One thread takes its blocks from the library and hands each to the receiver of the picture on the screen (`Receiver.take_audio`), where it is checked and given its time exactly as a picture's own sound is. The picture's connection is never asked for sound, and a receiver that gets its sound this way has no sound thread. The stream in the pipe is the same Matroska from the same writer, under the same rules (the sound track begins at 0, silence as time passes, the clock begins when the player has read its first picture).

- **A picture that changes size** ends as "changed" and is opened again, as ever. The sound's connection stays: it is not closed and not opened again.
- **The sound source is away** when a picture is opened: the picture plays as bare frames, without the half second's wait for sound. The helper looks for the source again every two seconds for as long as that picture plays; when it is there its first block ends the picture as "changed" and the panel opens it again, now with sound.
- **It is there and quiet** (a player that sends nothing between two clips): if it has had sound on this connection before, the new stream has a sound track from the start, in the form that sound had, and silence is written as time passes. The picture is not opened a second time when the sound comes. If it has never had sound on this connection, the picture begins as bare frames and the first sound opens it again, once. The remembered form is a guess until a block shows it: one in another form that comes before the pipe's form is fixed puts it right, and one that comes after is "changed".
- **It drops while a picture plays:** silence is written and the picture goes on. The library reconnects by itself.
- **Its rate or channels change:** "changed", and the picture is opened again.
- **Two senders under its id** (one name twice at one address): neither is played. The box does not pick one.
- **The picture on the screen is the sound source itself:** it plays its own sound on its own connection. The box never holds two connections to one sender for this.
- **When the connection exists.** From the first picture that needs it until the panel says nothing of NDI is on the screen, the input or the sound is switched off, or another source is chosen; and it ends by itself after 30 seconds with no picture to play under. With nothing of NDI on the screen the box holds no connection.
- **Changing the choice under a picture** ends it as "changed" ("where the sound comes from was changed"), and it is opened again in the new way.

**The delay.** Picture and sound from two senders share no clock, and the helper stamps each when it arrives. `ndi.sound_delay_ms` (-250 to 500, 0 by default) is set by ear and is the player's: the panel gives it to the player with the NDI stream that has sound (`Player.play_pipe(..., audio_delay)`), and the player sets mpv's `audio-delay` as a property before that load, on every mpv, exactly as it sets the narrowing. It is taken back on every road the narrowing is: before the next thing is loaded (`_undo_pipe_globals`), tried again if it could not be, and by a panel that started again under a player it did not set (`_widen`). So a clip never plays with it. Bare frames get none. A change while such a source plays is set at once (`Player.pipe_audio_delay`). Positive is sound later. The helper is told the number, says it back, and uses it for nothing. Not run against a real mpv: `audio-delay` is an ordinary number property of mpv's, and the test of it is against the player's fake.

**The settings and who may change them.** `"sound_from"` must be the id of a source in the same section's allowed list (`sources`); a settings file or an import whose id is not there is refused, and a hand-edited file with such an id is read as "no sound source". The Owner chooses with `POST /api/ndi` `{"action": "sound_from", "id": "<id>"}`: the id must be on the helper's list at that moment and not twins, and it joins the allowed list as a pad's source does. `{"action": "sound_from", "id": ""}` is each picture's own sound. Both switch sound on; `{"action": "sound", "on": false}` is no sound. `{"action": "sound_delay", "ms": n}` sets the delay. `forget` on the source that is the sound source is refused: "'X' is where this box's NDI sound comes from; choose another sound source first, then remove it". What `GET /api/ndi` adds: `sound_from`, `sound_delay_ms`, `sound_delay_range`, and `sound_source`: the source's name and address from the owner's own list, a state (`playing`, `ready`: on the network and connected to when a picture plays, `away`, `twins`, or `off` while sound is switched off) and whether sound arrives.

**What was added to the brief, each with a test in `tests/test_ndi_sound.py`.** Choosing a sound source switches sound on, and the page says "Sound was off: choosing a sound source switched it on" (`ApiSoundFromTest`, and the browser test). The states `ready` and `off` beside `playing`, `away` and `twins` (`SoundFromServiceTest`, `SoundFromPanelTest`). The connection is opened with a picture and gives itself back after 30 seconds without one (`SoundFeedTest`). A quiet sound source's remembered form gives the stream its sound track from the start (`SoundFeedTest`).

**A sender with sound and no picture.** Asked for a picture, it used to answer "sent no picture in 6 seconds", the same as a sender that is switched off. Now, when no picture came, the helper looks whether sound did: where the sound's own thread ran it knows; where none did (sound off, or the sound comes from the box's sound source) it asks the connection for one block, looks at its figures (`check_audio`) and gives it back with not a byte read. Then the answer is "X sends sound and no picture; choose it under 'Where sound comes from' on the NDI page", the source's row in `GET /api/ndi` carries `"sound_only": true` until it does send a picture (kept in the helper's memory, so until the helper starts again), and the page labels the row "sound only" and has "Use for sound" in place of Play. A sender is only known to be sound only after it was pressed once: the box connects to nothing it was not asked for. With one sound source chosen for the box, the sound the picture's receiver knows of is the sound source's, so only the picture's own connection is asked: a picture sender whose first picture is late is never called "sound only". A sender that sends neither in time answers "X sent no picture in 6 seconds, and no sound either (a sender of sound alone sends nothing while nothing plays into it)". **Said plainly, because it bends a rule above:** on this one road, a press that got no picture, the picture's connection is asked for one block of sound, also when sound is off; nothing of the block is read but its figures.

## Setting NDI up on a box (opt-in: one command, and what it changes)

The owner's answer of 2026-10-08: "install the helper only when NDI is wanted; one extra command at the box, alongside the runtime install."

**A box that was only installed** (`install/install.sh`, with or without `--offline`, an update from the panel, or the image) has, of NDI, only program files and the command `pvj-ndi-runtime` on the path. It has no `pvj-ndi` service, no `pvj-ndi` account or group, no folder `/run/pvj-ndi` or `/opt/pvj-ndi`, and nothing in the units of the panel and the player; the installer adds no avahi package for it; nothing of NDI listens, asks or announces on the network. The image build does not opt in. On such a box the NDI page says "NDI is not set up on this box" and shows the command below, the address list can already be filled in, and `GET /api/ndi` answers `"setup": false` without trying any socket.

**The one command.** The NDI runtime library, `libndi.so.6`, is proprietary; it is not in this repository and it is never in the image. Somebody with the right to accept NDI's licence fetches the NDI SDK for Linux from [ndi.video](https://ndi.video/) (the download asks you to agree to its licence), and at the box, as root, runs:

    sudo pvj-ndi-runtime install "/path/to/NDI SDK for Linux"      # the unpacked SDK folder, or the libndi.so.6 file itself

**Where to unpack the SDK.** In a folder only you can write: root's home, your own home, or a USB stick you brought. **Not in the media folder** (`/var/lib/pvj/video`) or anywhere under `/var/lib/pvj`: the panel's account can write there, and root would be reading a library from a place an unprivileged service controls. The command follows a link given as the file only to a plain name in the same folder, but it cannot know who else could have put the file there.

It first looks at everything that could refuse the setup, before anything on the box is changed: the install folder the installer wrote down, the unit's template, something that is not a plain file where one of its three files goes (a link or a folder; it says which, and "nothing was changed"), and a group or an account `pvj-ndi` that is already there (below). Then it does two things, in this order, and can be run again to repair or to put a newer library in place:

1. **The library.** It picks the file for this board from the SDK, checks it (see below) and copies it to `/opt/pvj-ndi/libndi.so.6`. A file that is refused ends the command there: nothing else is changed, and the folder `/opt/pvj-ndi` is not left behind either.
2. **The helper** (`pvj/ndisetup.py`, root's code for this and nothing else):
   - installs `avahi-daemon` and `libavahi-client3` with `apt-get` if they are missing (the library finds senders through them; Raspberry Pi OS has both already). Without a network this is said and the rest goes on; senders are then not found until the packages are there;
   - makes the system group and account `pvj-ndi` (no home, no shell, in no other group). An account of that name that is already there and is not that (it is root under another name, shares its number with another account, has a login shell, or is in another group) is refused, not repaired, and so is a group `pvj-ndi` that is root's group under another name, shares its number with another group (an account made into it would be in that other group, `pvj` for one), has members, or is somebody else's own group. Members are refused because whoever is in the group can enter the helper's folder; the panel and the player are let in by their units, never by membership. All of that is found out before the library is copied or a package installed. Should the account come out of `useradd` other than asked (a box whose own rules for new accounts add a group or a shell), the command stops, says that the account was made a moment ago and how to remove it;
   - writes `/etc/systemd/system/pvj-ndi.service` (from `pvj/systemd/pvj-ndi.service`, which travels with the program) and two drop-ins, `pvj-web.service.d/50-pvj-ndi.conf` and `pvj-player.service.d/50-pvj-ndi.conf`, each adding the extra group `pvj-ndi` to that service; the panel's also sets `PVJ_NDI_DIR`, which is how the panel knows the box is set up;
   - reloads systemd, enables and starts `pvj-ndi`, looks for three seconds whether it stays up, and restarts the panel and the player if they are running, because an extra group only reaches a process when it starts. **The screen goes dark for a moment**; do it before a show, not during one.

**What it says at the end, and what a failure leaves.** "NDI is set up on this box: the helper pvj-ndi is installed and was running 3 seconds after its start", or, if the helper was started and did not stay up, "NDI is set up on this box, but the helper pvj-ndi is NOT running" with where to look; the setup is then left in place so that it can be looked at on the box, and the command ends with an error. If writing one of the three files (a full or read-only disk, something in its place), reloading systemd, enabling or starting the helper itself fails on a box that was not set up before, the unit and the drop-ins are taken away again and the command says "the setup failed and was undone: this box is not set up for NDI"; what stays then is the library, the account and group, and any package that was installed (`sudo pvj-ndi-runtime remove` takes the library off). On a box that was already set up, a failed run leaves it set up.

**On a box with the read-only root** (`sudo pvj-rootfs enable`, see `pvj/README.md`): while it is active everything written since the last start is in memory and is gone at the next restart, and that includes the library, the account and the helper's unit. The command says so when it sees it, at its start and at its end, and goes on. To set NDI up so that it stays: `sudo pvj-rootfs disable`, restart, run the command, `sudo pvj-rootfs enable`, restart. The same holds for `remove` and for any update.

**Why this command and not an installer flag.** A box made from the image, or updated from the panel, has no copy of the installer on it: only `pvj/` and `bin/` are kept. A flag on `install.sh` could not be "one command at the box" there. `pvj-ndi-runtime` is on every box's path, was already the root command for the library, and the unit it writes travels with the program.

**An update keeps an opted-in box opted in.** The panel's drop-in is the mark. (The panel itself reads `PVJ_NDI_DIR` from its environment, which that drop-in sets. Root could also set it in `/etc/pvj/pvj.env`; on a box without the helper the page would then say "the helper is not running", which is the wrong words and nothing more. The installer never writes it there.) `install.sh` (and so every update) looks for it; where it is there, the unit and the two drop-ins are written again by the newly installed program (`pvj-ndi-runtime refresh`, the same code that wrote them, so there is one writer), the helper stays enabled, and it is restarted (with `--no-start`, as an update from the panel runs it, only if it was running). Where the mark is not there, the installer does nothing about NDI, with one exception: a helper's unit, the player's drop-in or the link that enabled the unit, without the mark (a setup that was cut short, or a box that ran this branch while it still put the helper on every box; no such box is known) is disabled and removed, and the installer says "removing an NDI helper that nobody set up on this box". The account, if there is one, stays. **So a box that ran an earlier build of this branch loses its helper at the next install or update and needs the opt-in command again.** A link where the mark goes is not an opt-in either, but systemd would read a drop-in through it, so the installer removes the link (the link itself, never what it points to) and says so with a WARNING. If one of these cannot be removed because a folder is in its place, the installer says which with a WARNING and goes on; it never stops an install or an update for it.

**A rollback** (`pvj-update rollback`, or an update that fails its health check) switches the program back and writes no units. Between two releases that both have the NDI input, the updater restarts a running helper together with the panel and the player, so all three run the same release. Back to a release from before the NDI input, the helper's unit and the drop-ins stay and the helper's program is gone: the unit has `ConditionPathExists=` on its program, so it is skipped, once, and is not started again and again (without that line it would fail every two seconds into the kept journal). The command `pvj-ndi-runtime` is then a link to nothing and cannot be run. The panel and the player start as before (the group they name still exists). To take the helper off such a box by hand:

    sudo systemctl disable --now pvj-ndi.service
    sudo rm -f /etc/systemd/system/pvj-web.service.d/50-pvj-ndi.conf /etc/systemd/system/pvj-player.service.d/50-pvj-ndi.conf /etc/systemd/system/pvj-ndi.service
    sudo systemctl daemon-reload
    sudo systemctl try-restart pvj-web pvj-player

or update forward again, after which `sudo pvj-ndi-runtime remove` works. Nothing of this has been tried on a box; the CI runner tries the skipped unit with the helper's program moved away.

**Opting out, and uninstalling.**

    sudo pvj-ndi-runtime status      # says whether the library is in place and whether the box is set up
    sudo pvj-ndi-runtime remove      # opt out

`remove` stops and disables the helper, removes its unit, the two drop-ins and the library, and restarts the panel and the player without the extra group. If one of those files cannot be removed (something else is in its place), it says which, goes on with the rest and the library, and ends with an error. It leaves the account and group `pvj-ndi` (`sudo deluser pvj-ndi; sudo delgroup pvj-ndi` removes them) and the avahi packages, which may have been on the box before and which other things may use; it says so. So if the opt-in installed `avahi-daemon`, it keeps running after the opt-out until someone removes it. A box with `avahi-daemon` answers to its name by mDNS whether or not NDI is set up; that is avahi, not NDI. `install.sh --uninstall` removes the helper's unit and the drop-ins with everything else and leaves `/opt/pvj-ndi` (the owner's copy of the library), the account and the group; `--uninstall --purge` removes `/opt/pvj-ndi` too. A box installed again after an uninstall has not opted in.

**Before installing it: NDI's licence may not cover a box like this one: read it before you install the runtime. This is not legal advice.** The agreement names kinds of product its free licence does not cover, and a small box built for one job may be one of them; see "The SDK License Agreement itself" below. The owner of the box decides, and asks NDI if in doubt.

**The checks on the library file.** The command picks the file for this board from the SDK's `lib/` folder, refuses a file built for another kind of processor and says which ("this file is for x86_64, this box is aarch64"), and copies it to `/opt/pvj-ndi/libndi.so.6` (root's, read-only for every service). It needs root on the box (a keyboard or SSH), on purpose: see "Why there is no upload button". A link given as the file is followed only where the SDK itself has one: to a plain name in the same folder (`libndi.so.6` to `libndi.so.6.3.2`), at most two links deep; a link that points into another folder, to a name with a folder in it, to nothing, or on to more links is refused, and the file that is then opened is opened without following a link. It does not wait on a pipe, reads no more than the size it allows, and keeps its copy private until every check has passed.

If the library is taken away by hand on a box that is set up, the page says "The NDI runtime is not on this box yet" and shows the same command; its switch, its list and its address form are on the same page.

## How it works

    sender --NDI--> libndi.so.6 (in pvj-ndi, its own account and sandbox)
                      | UYVY frames, whole frames only, newest wins
                      v
                  /run/pvj-ndi-pipe/ndi.fifo  --read by-->  the one mpv (rawvideo demuxer, uyvy422)

- `pvj-ndi.service` (only on a box that was set up for NDI) runs `pvj/ndi.py` as the account `pvj-ndi`. It loads the library with `ctypes` (standard library; nothing is compiled), asks it for the senders on the network, and, when the panel asks for one, receives its frames.
- Frames are asked for in the library's "fastest" format, which is 8-bit UYVY (UYVY plus a separate alpha plane when the sender has alpha; the alpha plane is not read). No colour conversion happens in Python or in the library.
- The helper writes raw frames into a pipe; the player plays the pipe with mpv's raw video demuxer, as it already does for the HDMI capture input (`pvj/capture.py`, measured on the Pi 4 at 1080p30). The player never loads the library.
- The size and frame rate are not known until the first frame. So: the panel asks the helper to open a source; the helper connects and waits up to 6 seconds for a frame; it answers with the width, height and rate; the panel loads the pipe in the player with exactly those; the helper starts writing when the player opens the pipe.
- **One pipe for every source, so the order is made** (2026-10-12). Until the review's M3 every source had a pipe of its own, and that alone kept the player still on the last source apart from the writer of the next. Now the pipe is root's, made once and never unlinked, and the same pipe carries one source after another. A reader of a pipe cannot be asked who it is: the writer of the next source can only tell the old reader from the new one by the pipe having had no reader in between. So this is the order, and each step waits for the one before: the panel tells the player to let go of the pipe if the pipe is what it loaded last (`Player.release_pipe`: mpv's `stop`, which also ends a load that is still opening, and then mpv is asked until it says `idle-active`, for 1.5 seconds at most: the panel's own confirmation that the old load is over); the helper closes the last source's writer (the old reader is given the end); it connects and waits for a first frame, with the new writer held back; it looks at the pipe until it has seen it without a reader (opening it for writing without waiting is refused exactly then; `Service._clear_pipe`); only then does it answer the panel and let the writer go. The panel loads the pipe after that answer and plays one source at a time, so the reader the writer then meets is the one for this source, and a pipe nobody held holds no bytes of the source before. This costs no time when the player lets go while the helper is still connecting. The player is TOLD to let go because it cannot be counted on to do so by itself at the end of a stream: on the Pi on 2026-10-12 (mpv 0.40, sound on), before any of this, a source whose size changed with every clip was opened again on the same pipe and twice out of twice ended at once with "the player stopped reading the input", having shown one frame: the player was still reading the pipe seconds after the old writer had closed, took the new stream's first frame into the old one, and was then closed by the panel's load. Tested with a real pipe and readers that hold their end after the end of the stream, stop reading with part of a frame in the pipe, or open late for the load before (`PipeReuseTest`), and through the panel with a stand-in that holds the pipe until it is told to let go, over four changes of size one after the other (`PlayerLetsGoTest`); before the change, in the same tests, the next source's frames went into the old stream, the source ended with "the player stopped reading the input" and the player's new load waited on a pipe nobody wrote. The helper gets no new power by this: it opens and closes a pipe it may write anyway and writes nothing while it looks.
- **A still picture with sound on the box's own player: the sound device was opened and never given a sample. Cause found by hand and changed; NOT yet seen cured on the box (2026-10-12).** After the change above the box's mpv had 2 tracks, sound parameters, the ALSA device and `core-idle` false, and ALSA's own file (`/proc/asound/card6/pcm0p/sub0/status`) said `state: PREPARED`, `hw_ptr 0`, `appl_ptr 0` for as long as it was looked at (25 seconds, twice a second); `audio-pts` was unavailable, or after sound was switched on under the source a number that stood (4.703, with `appl_ptr 4800`). The hand-run of the day had not shown it because its sound began at the picture's time. With the first sound 11 ms after the picture, as on the box, mpv 0.40 by hand says: `delaying audio start 0.011000 vs. 0.000000, diff=0.011000`, then `playback restart complete @ 0.000000, audio=ready, video=playing`, and `starting audio playback` only when the pipe ends. mpv does not begin the sound before the picture has reached the first sound's time (`player/audio.c`, `audio_start_ao`), and a picture that stands never does. The stream is now also loaded with `initial-audio-sync=no`; by hand, with the first sound at 0.011 and at 4.7 seconds: `starting audio playback` at 0.237 and `audio-pts` going on at real time. What is not shown: ALSA's pointer moving. The hand-run cannot open a sound device on the box (its account is not in the audio group), so that is for the box's own player after the next install: `state: RUNNING` and `hw_ptr` going on by 48000 a second, for two minutes. The end-to-end checker now wants the sound's own clock (`audio-pts`) to go on wherever sound must be there; before, the picture's clock or two tracks were enough. It could still not have caught this on CI: the stand-in library's first sound has the picture's time.
- **Picture and sound in step: what was measured, what was changed, and what no measurement here can show (2026-10-12).** *What `initial-audio-sync=no` changes* (mpv 0.40, `player/audio.c`): with it on, mpv does not begin the sound before the picture has reached the first sound's time (`delaying audio start 0.211111 vs. 0.000000`) and cuts off sound from before the first picture; with it off it does neither and begins the sound at once. Afterwards nothing differs: the picture follows the sound's clock by the times in the stream (`video-sync=audio`). *Measured* with the helper's real `Receiver` fed in real time (1280 x 720 at 30 a second, 100 ms blocks) into the Pi's mpv 0.40 without a screen or sound device, asked once a second for two minutes a case: a moving source with its sound, `avsync` at most 0.001 after the first two seconds, no picture dropped, `total-avsync-change` 0.002 to 0.007; the sender's sound beginning 4.7 seconds late (the helper writes silence), 0.000; no new picture for the first 0.3 seconds, 0.000; a new stream at another size, 0.000; a clip paused for 20 seconds, `avsync` reads 0.34 while the picture stands (the number is the last one it had) and 0.000 at the first sample 0.2 seconds after it moves again. One case was out of step: the first sound stamped 0.211 after the first picture. mpv began both at once: `avsync` 0.206, over 40 ms for 1.8 seconds, 0 after 3.4 (it moves the picture gently: `total-avsync-change` 0.206). With `initial-audio-sync` on it was in step from the start but 0.39 seconds behind the helper for good, not 0.20. *Changed in the stream, so that this cannot be asked of the player:* (1) the sound track begins where the picture's does: the part of the first block from before the stream is cut off (written whole at 0 it was up to a block's length early), and a first block that begins later is led up to by silence from 0. Measured again: 0.000 from the first sample. (2) The stream's time 0 is when its first picture has gone through the pipe, not when the pipe was opened: a player that opened it and read nothing for 4.7 seconds was given a first picture at 0 and everything else seconds later. (3) Silence is written as time passes when neither picture nor sound comes, so the sound's times never jump: after 6 seconds without sound under a still the stream had one second of silence and a jump of five, and mpv takes a jump of 5 seconds or more in the sound's times for a new beginning, starts both tracks again and waits for a picture that never comes (CI's "long gap" case on mpv 0.40: core idle, no sound). All fifteen cases of `tests/test_ndi_mpv.py` then pass on the Pi's mpv 0.40, run by hand. `initial-audio-sync=no` stays: with it on, a still whose sound begins a moment late (11 ms on the box) would wait again, and it buys nothing once the stream begins whole. *The 4.7 seconds* (`audio-pts` 4.703 against `time-pos` 0 after sound was switched on, on the build before): the stream's clock began when the pipe was OPENED, and a player that opened it and then read nothing for seconds (its screen and sound device being set up) was given a first picture at 0 and everything after it at the seconds it had taken. That is change (2); the test of it gives the old helper a reader that stalls 4.7 seconds and finds its first sound at 3.5 seconds (a second of sound is kept). It is not a still's fault alone: a moving source's first new picture got the same seconds. Not proven to be what the box did that once (the same switch now gives a first sound at about 0.1 seconds); a second candidate that fits less well is the jump of change (3). *Proven without mpv:* `tests/test_ndi_sound.py` marks every picture and every block of sound with the sender's own time and compares their times in the written stream: within 2 ms for blocks of 1024 to 12000 samples, within 25 ms when the sound comes unevenly, and after a reader that stalls at the start. (The first two of those pass on the helper as it was and are guards; the stalled reader, the silence up to a late first sound, and no jump in the sound's times each fail on it.) *What none of this shows.* `avsync` is mpv's belief about how well it keeps to the stream's times; it is 0.000 also when the stream itself is wrong. The stream's times are when each thing ARRIVED at the helper: the sender's own time for each (NDI gives one) is not used, so whatever the library takes longer to hand up a picture than a block of sound is in the stream as an offset, and it was not measured (the one moving sender on the network delivered nothing to a second receiver in 25 seconds). After mpv, the screen and the amplifier each add their own delay. Two senders share no clock at all. The check that counts takes a minute: play a clip with a clap or a flash-and-beep in VLC through Scan Converter and watch the box's screen; if lips and sound are apart, the box needs a setting to move the sound (`sound_delay_ms`, planned, not built), and the number is whatever makes the clap one.
- **After the review of the mended hand-over (2026-10-12, follow-ups).** The stream's time 0 is when the player has READ its first picture, not when the picture was written: a pipe on Linux holds up to a megabyte, more than a whole picture of a small source (640 x 360 is 460 KB), so such a picture was "through the pipe" while the player had read nothing, and the clock began seconds before the player did (CI's runners and the Pi, the test of the stalled reader: a first sound at 3.533 s; on macOS, whose pipes hold 64 KB, it passed). The helper now asks the pipe how much is unread (`FIONREAD`) and waits for none. A 1080p picture is four pipes long and was right before. With `cache-pause=no`, a pipe that runs dry for a moment (a slow sender, a busy box) gives a stutter in picture or sound and then goes on at the same lateness; it no longer gives a pause "for buffering" that leaves the sound late for good. The pause between tries to show a source again is three seconds again only once the source has been on the screen for 30 seconds without a break; one look that found it "playing" used to be enough, so a player that read the pipe for a few seconds and gave up was stopped, opened and loaded every few seconds for ever. A source whose mpv became another process (it fell, or the panel ended it) is shown again like one that lost its reader; before, it ended with "the player stopped reading the input". Once mpv has said it is idle the panel's record no longer says that the NDI pipe plays, and a first press that is refused at the look at the pipe leaves its reason on the page. **Not built, and why: asking the helper to connect to the new source BEFORE the player lets go of the old one**, so that a press on a listed source that then fails to open (about 10 seconds of waiting for a first picture) would cost nothing. It needs the helper to hold two connections at once (the old one still writing, the new one waiting for its first picture: twice the decoding on a Pi, inside a 512 MB and three-core bound that was reasoned for one), an answer in two steps (connected; then, after the player let go, "go"), and every way either step can fail. That is a second hand-over protocol beside the one that was just mended and measured. What is refused before the screen is touched stays what it is now: a source that is not on the list.
- **"Playing" is the player's word too, not only the helper's (2026-10-12).** mpv gives the pipe as its `path` from the moment it is told to load it, also while it has not opened the stream and nothing is on the screen; the helper meanwhile writes and says "playing" or "still". So the panel asks mpv one thing more: `track-list/count`, which on mpv 0.40 was 0 for exactly as long as the open lasted and 2 from "Opening done" on (measured by hand on the Pi). No track 5 seconds after the load (`Player.pipe_stuck`): the status says `ndi_opening`, the watch shows the source again once, and if the player has not opened it then either the source is ended with "the player did not open the NDI picture, also not when it was given it again" and the helper lets go of it (the end of the stream is what frees a player stuck on it). Not judged by a moving clock: while a picture stands, mpv's `time-pos` stands with it by design.
- **When the player does not confirm that it let go, the helper's source is closed first and the player asked again**; only then is the press refused (the reason stays on the page). Before, the refusal came first, the old writer went on feeding the stuck player, and every press was refused for ever (the review of 2026-10-12, high 1). A `stop` that mpv does not answer is not "let go" unless mpv is not running. A press on a source that is not on the network now is refused before the player is asked to let go, so what plays stays.
- **A player that will not let go is never written over.** If mpv does not say it is idle within 1.5 seconds of the stop, the panel does not ask the helper at all ("the player did not let go of the NDI picture before; not starting another over it"). If the helper does not see the pipe without a reader within 2 seconds (`PIPE_CLEAR_SECONDS`), it lets the new source go again, writes nothing and answers "the player still holds the NDI pipe from the picture before". Either way the press is answered with that reason, and for a source that is wanted the watch tries again (3 seconds, then 6, and so on) with the reason on the page. Whether mpv has the pipe is asked of mpv itself (its `path`) as well as of what the panel remembers having loaded, so a panel that started again under a playing source still has its mpv let go. And if a writer does lose its reader later, the panel shows the source again by itself: "the player stopped reading" means "something else was played" only when the panel did load something else; while the NDI pipe is still what the panel loaded last (`Player.pipe_is`, the panel's own record, never the helper's word) the watch opens the source again, less and less often if it keeps happening.
- **A sender that changes size or rate**: the helper stops writing (the raw pipe has one fixed layout) and says "changed"; the panel, which looks at the helper once a second while NDI is on screen, opens the source again and reloads the pipe. The picture is gone for the second or two that takes.
- **A still picture** (a test pattern, a paused output, a slide): the sender sends a frame and then nothing new. That is normal and is left alone: the page says "On the screen, a still picture", and nothing is reloaded however long it lasts. The frame is written once as bare frames; in a stream with sound a picture that stands is written once more (see "Not verified" for why). (For one day the helper wrote such a frame a second time, on a reading of mpv that the Pi then showed to be wrong: the still picture was on the monitor without it. It was taken out again and never ran on a box.)
- **A snapshot of an NDI source that the player cannot make** (Pads, "Take snapshot") is answered with 409 and "no snapshot of this NDI source could be made (the player could not copy its picture, which happens with a still source); the picture on the screen is not affected", and the page shows those words; it was a 503 with mpv's own error. On the Pi the still picture was on the monitor while the snapshot failed, so the words do not put the screen in doubt. Why mpv refuses is not known (step N12).
- **A live source plays at speed 1.** The Mix screen's speed is for clips. When an NDI source, the capture input or a stream is played, the player is set to speed 1; the Mix speed is kept (and can be changed meanwhile: it is stored, not applied) and is given back to the player when the next clip is played. The Mix screen says so beside the slider while a live source plays. The hold ends when Stop is pressed, and when the status (asked about once a second by an open page) finds that nothing live is playing any more, three seconds or more after the hold began: a stream that ran out, a sender that went, a player that was started again. It ends only once the player has really taken the Mix speed; until then it is tried again. Looking at the hold and setting the player's speed are one step under one lock, so a speed request and a play at the same moment cannot leave a live source at the Mix speed. OSC, MIDI and the lighting desk all set the speed through the same request (`/api/control`). The multi-box follower (`pvj/sync.py`) sets the player's speed by itself to stay in step with a server's clip; it follows clips, not live sources, and was not changed.
- **A sender that goes away**: only when the library itself reports that the connection dropped does the page say "The connection to the sender dropped; waiting for it". The helper keeps the connection (the library reconnects by itself), the screen holds the last frame, and when frames return they continue; if they return in another size, see above. If the helper has nothing open (it started again, or the sender was gone at a reload), the panel tries to show the source again after 3 seconds, then 6, 12 and so on up to once a minute, and writes the reason in the log once.
- **The helper starts again under a source** (a crash in the library; or its own exit half a second after the discovery servers were changed, which is the only way the library reads its file again). The player is given the end and the screen is black. systemd starts the helper 2 seconds later; the panel's watch (once a second) tells the new one the settings and asks for the source, which the new helper has to find on the network first; a try that comes too early is repeated 3 seconds later, then 6. So the gap is about 3 seconds at best and 6 to 7 when the first try is too early; how long the real library takes to find a sender again is not measured. A pad that holds the source is not lit while the screen is black (it goes by what the player plays) and is lit again when the picture is back. Tested against the faked library (`HelperRestartTest`): it comes back every time, also when the helper goes again in the middle of the watch's own try. Not brought back: a source that was pressed for the first time and was still connecting when the helper went. That press is answered "the NDI helper (pvj-ndi) is not running" and has to be made again; if another source was playing before the press, that one comes back.
- **A source is a name on a machine.** The id the panel plays by is made from the checked name and the address of the machine that announces it (not the port, which changes when a sender restarts). A second machine announcing the same name is another row and can never take the place of the one that was chosen. The price: a sender whose address changes (DHCP) becomes a new row and has to be chosen again; staying with the machine was preferred to following the name.
- **Too many senders**: at most 64 are listed. Which ones does not depend on the order they are announced in: the one on the screen first, then senders at an address the owner added, then the rest by name. The page says when the list was cut and that adding the sender's address brings it in.
- **A source that ends by itself** (a format this input does not show, or the player moved on) is no longer "what plays"; the page keeps the reason until something is played again. **Never nothing playing and no reason** (2026-10-12; on the Pi a source was gone with both empty): a source that ended keeps its reason whatever it was, "the player stopped reading the input" included; and a source that is wanted but not on the screen while the watch brings it back (the helper away, the sender not found, the player lost the pipe) is shown as "NAME stopped: the reason; trying again".
- **Something else is played while a source is still connecting** (it can take seconds): the later choice stands; the source is let go and the screen is not touched. Everything that loads or clears the player through the panel's own calls cancels a connecting source first, and waits if the NDI pipe is being loaded at that very moment. Two paths load the player without going through that door (a client box following its server in "Boxes in step", and a shader swap); for those the pipe simply loses its reader and the helper lets the source go.
- **Slow screen, fast sender**: the helper holds one frame; a newer one replaces it and the old one is counted as dropped. A frame is never written in part. The counts (received, shown, dropped here, dropped by the library) are on the page and in the status route.
- **Interlaced senders are refused**, with a plain message ("the source sends interlaced video, which this input does not show; set the sender to progressive"). That covers all three interlaced frame types the library has: a frame of two fields woven together (value 0), and single fields (2 and 3). Only a progressive frame (1) is shown. Why not have the library de-interlace: as NDI's documentation of receiving describes it (in this project's words), the library de-interlaces when the receiver's `allow_video_fields` setting is false, and the "fastest" colour format always implies that setting to be true. So de-interlacing in the library means giving up the fastest format (the alternative with UYVY hands frames with alpha over as BGRA, converted), for senders that Resolume and MadMapper are not. The helper therefore asks for fastest with fields allowed, said outright, and refuses what is not progressive. **To be confirmed on a device with an interlaced sender** (step N8): which of the three values really arrive, and that the message is what the page shows.
- **Sound** is taken from the library on a thread of its own and travels in the same pipe as the picture, each with its time: see "Sound" above. With sound off, or from a source that sends none, the pipe carries bare frames as described here.

## Routes and roles

| Route | Role | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GET /api/ndi` | live (an Operator; a Guest has no NDI page, D80) | Whether the box is set up for NDI (`setup`, with the command when it is not), the runtime (present, version, a problem in words), the helper, the sources found, the saved addresses, what plays and its counts |
| `POST /api/ndi` `{"action": "add_address", "address": "192.168.1.20"}` | full | Also look for senders at this address |
| `POST /api/ndi` `{"action": "remove_address", "address": "..."}` | full | Forget it |
| `POST /api/ndi` `{"action": "sound", "on": true}` | full | Play a source's sound with its picture, or not |
| `POST /api/ndi` `{"action": "sound_from", "id": "<id>"}` | full | One sound source for every NDI picture (a source on the list now; it becomes allowed), or `""` for each picture's own sound. Switches sound on |
| `POST /api/ndi` `{"action": "sound_delay", "ms": 120}` | full | Move the sound against the picture, -250 to 500 ms (the player's `audio-delay` for the NDI stream with sound only) |
| `POST /api/play` `{"ndi": "<id>"}` | live | Show a source. The id comes from `GET /api/ndi`; a name is never taken |
| `POST /api/pads` `{"bank", "index", "label"?, "ndi": "<id>"}` | live | Put a source on a pad: one that is allowed already, or one on the list now, which this makes allowed |
| `POST /api/play` `{"pad": [bank, index]}` on such a pad | live; a Guest is refused (403) | Show the pad's source, after the checks under "Pads" |
| `POST /api/ndi` `{"action": "forget", "id": "<id>"}` | full | Take a source off the allowed list |
| `POST /api/ndi` `{"action": "add_group" or "remove_group", "group": "Leyline"}` | full | Which NDI groups the box looks in |
| `POST /api/ndi` `{"action": "add_discovery" or "remove_discovery", "server": "192.168.1.5:5959"}` | full | Which NDI Discovery Servers the box asks |
| `POST /api/ndi` `{"action": "own_discovery", "on": true}` | full | Also ask the discovery server this box runs |
| `GET /api/ndi/discovery` | full, never through remote support | The discovery server on this box: set up or not, what listens, what to tell senders |
| `POST /api/ndi/discovery` `{"network": "wired" or "wifi" or "all", "port": 5959}` | full, never through remote support | Which network it listens on, and the port |
| `POST /api/modules/ndi-discovery` `{"enabled": true}` | full, never through remote support | Switch the discovery server on or off |

Settings: `"ndi": {"addresses": []}`, since D87 `"sound_from": "<id>"` and `"sound_delay_ms": n` when they were chosen (see "One sound source for the whole box"), and, once someone has used the switch, `"sound": true` or `false` (no schema change since 2026-10-11: the key is read with defaults everywhere, as `room` is, so a file from this branch loads on master and a master file loads here; the branch had bumped to 15 before, and the test Pi keeps one such file as a backup; sound needed no new key either, a file without it means off). The switch is the module `inputs-ndi` (off by default, D43).

**On a box that is not set up** the routes answer cleanly and at once: `GET /api/ndi` gives `"setup": false`, `"helper": false`, no sources and the command; the addresses can be added and removed (they are plain settings, kept for when the box is set up); `POST /api/play` with an NDI source answers 409, "NDI is not set up on this box", with the command. The helper's socket is never tried there.

**Remote support: the same as Streams** (the owner's answer of 2026-10-08). A full-access support session may read and change the NDI addresses through the tunnel exactly as it may change Streams: neither route is on the list of what support can never do (`support.REMOTE_DENY`), neither handler has a rule of its own for the tunnel, and both ask the same roles. `tests/test_ndi_setup.py` (`TunnelSameAsStreamsTest`) pins that the two stay the same. What a support session cannot do is set NDI up or put a library on the box: that is root at the box, for everyone.

## Safety

Everything that arrives from the network is treated as hostile, and the code that reads it is a closed-source library, so the design assumes the helper can be taken over by a bad packet and limits what that would give.

- **Opt-in.** A box where nobody ran the command has no helper, no account for it, and nothing of NDI on the network. What follows is about a box that was set up.
- **Its own account and group** (`pvj-ndi`). It is not in group `pvj`, so it cannot reach the player's control socket (mpv's control channel can run programs), the PIN, the settings or the media. The player and the panel are each given the extra group `pvj-ndi` (by the two drop-ins the opt-in writes), which lets them read the helper's folder and nothing else. A unit that names a group the box does not have is not started by systemd, which is why the group is in drop-ins and not in the units the installer writes for every box.
- **Its sandbox** (`pvj/systemd/pvj-ndi.service`): no capabilities, no devices, a read-only system, no home folders, a `/tmp` of its own held to 8 MB, two writable places (`/run/pvj-ndi`, and root's pipe in `/run/pvj-ndi-pipe`), at most 512 MB of memory (what it writes to `/run` counts), 96 tasks and three of a Pi 4's four cores (so the player always has one; memory and processor chosen by reasoning and not measured; the tasks were 64 until 2026-10-12 and were raised after a measurement by hand, see "One sound source for the whole box"), and **network addresses limited to private ranges, link-local and multicast** (`IPAddressDeny=any` with an allow list), which is meant to keep the library from talking to the internet at all. `tests/test_units.py` pins every line; that the lines do on a device what they say is step N9 of the device steps, not yet done.
- **Its pipe is root's** (since 2026-10-11, the review's M3): made once by the tmpfiles lines `pvj-ndi-runtime install` writes (`/run/pvj-ndi-pipe` 0750 root:pvj-ndi; `ndi.fifo` 0660 root:pvj-ndi), written by the helper through its group, never unlinked by it, and not replaceable by it (it cannot make a name in root's folder). The panel refuses to load the pipe unless it is a pipe owned by root in a folder of root's that nobody else may write (`Input.check_pipe`). Before this the helper made the pipe in its own folder and could have swapped it for a link between the panel's look and the player's opening.
- **What a taken-over helper can reach on the network** (M3): the box's own LAN address is inside the private ranges the unit allows, so the panel (as an unpaired stranger), OSC (when on and unlocked, D78) and sync (when on) are in its reach as they are in any LAN client's; 127.0.0.1 is not. The whole boundary is in SECURITY.md; what to try on the Pi against it is N21 in `tools/DEVICE-TESTING.md`.

- **The helper answers only the panel's account** on its socket (`SO_PEERCRED`), with the same small server the other helpers use.
- **The panel does not trust the helper's answers.** One deadline bounds a whole question and answer, however slowly the answer trickles in; an answer nested deeper than a real one is refused before it is read; every name, number and sentence in it is checked again (and scrubbed of control and direction-changing characters) before it reaches the page; and no answer, or fault, of the helper can stop the panel from starting.
- **Off means no NDI code running.** When the module is switched off after having been on, the helper ends and systemd starts a fresh one that loads nothing until the module is on again.
- **What ends the helper, and how often it is started again.** The unit starts it again 2 seconds after it ends, without a limit. Two things end it: the module being switched off with the library loaded (on purpose, see above), and a crash inside the library. A library that is missing, or that does not load, ends nothing: the helper stays up, loads nothing and says what is wrong on the page. A library that crashes while loading would be started again each time the panel asks for it, which is while somebody has the NDI page open with the module on; each start is small and bounded by the unit's limits, and it writes a line or two to the journal (kept, 64 MB at most). The 2 seconds were left as they are on purpose: a slower restart would also slow the clean start after switching off, and a different treatment by exit status cannot tell a crash in a closed library from anything else. If device step N3 shows the library crashing on load, switch the module off (that stops the asking) and send the journal.
- **The system bus stays reachable** from the helper, and has to: NDI's library finds senders through `libavahi-client`, which talks to `avahi-daemon` over D-Bus and no other way.
- **The pipe.** Root makes it, once, and nobody removes it (see "Its pipe is root's" above; until 2026-10-11 the helper made and removed it and the panel's look was only a tripwire). Before the player is told to read it, the panel looks that it is a pipe of root's in a folder of root's. mpv's own source (`stream/stream_file.c`, read on 2026-10-07 from its master branch) opens a file for reading without blocking and waits for data in a way a later command interrupts, so a helper that never writes does not hold the player; whether the older mpv on Raspberry Pi OS Bookworm (0.35) does the same was not determined and is step N11.
- **What a helper that was taken over can make the player parse, and what it cannot** (corrected after the review of 2026-10-08; the first text here said "raw video only", which stopped being true the day sound was built).
  - **With sound off** (the switch, or a box whose owner never wanted sound): bare frames. mpv is told the size, the rate and the format and reads the pipe as pixels and nothing else, whatever the helper writes and whatever it answers. That is what it always was. The panel, not the helper, decides this: the pipe is read as Matroska only when the owner's switch is on AND the helper says the pipe carries sound; with the switch off a helper that answers "matroska" is read as bare frames (checked twice, in the panel's handle on the helper and again at the call to the player). So switching sound off really is the way back to raw frames.
  - **With sound on**, the player reads the helper's bytes with **mpv's Matroska reader**. That reader is now in reach of a taken-over helper, which it was not before: every part of `demux_mkv.c` that parses the heading, tracks, clusters, block lacing, the header-stripping and zlib "content compression" of blocks, and whatever tags, chapters, attachments and seek entries a stream cares to carry, runs in the player's account on bytes the helper chose. This is more than before, it is the cost of sound, and it is accepted and written down here rather than argued away. A fault in that reader would give the helper what the player has: group `pvj`, the clips, the screen, and mpv's own control socket.
  - **What is closed on top of that.** For that one load the player is narrowed first (`Player.MKV_ONLY`, set as properties before the load on every mpv; a property mpv refuses refuses the load; taken back before the next thing is loaded, and tried again if it could not be). `vd=rawvideo,-` and `ad=pcm_f32le,-`: a decoder list that ends in `-` has no fallback (`common/codecs.c`, `mp_select_decoders`, read at v0.35.0, v0.37.0 and v0.40.0 and the same in all three: a decoder is taken only if it is on the list and is for the track's codec), so a track that names any other codec, a cover picture in an attachment among them, is not decoded: no libavcodec decoder but raw video and float PCM is reachable from the helper. `sid=no`: no subtitle track is chosen, so no subtitle decoder runs and no font from the stream is loaded (`embeddedfonts=no` says the second half again). `ordered-chapters=no`: chapters that name other files are not followed. `cover-art-auto=no`: a stream that claims to have no picture does not send mpv looking for a picture file beside the pipe, in a folder the helper can write to. Already off for everything the player plays: `sub-auto`, `audio-file-auto`, `access-references`, scripts.
  - **What that leaves open:** the Matroska reader itself (above), and the two decoders that stay, raw video and float PCM, which copy more than they compute. mpv says "Excluding codecs is deprecated" in its log for the `-` at these versions; it works at all three and a later mpv may want another spelling, which the real-mpv test in CI would show.
- **Names**: a source name from the network is shown and used only if it is valid UTF-8, 1 to 128 characters, with no control, format (bidirectional overrides, zero width) or line-separator characters. It is written one way (accented letters composed, every kind of space as a plain one), so two rows cannot look alike while being different, and a pile of accents on one letter is refused. At most 64 sources are kept. The panel plays by an id, a hash of the name and the announcing machine made by the helper, never by a string from the request.
- **Frames**: width, height, line stride and format are checked against fixed bounds (16 to 3840 by 16 to 2160, an even width, a stride of at least two bytes per pixel and at most a bounded excess) before a single byte is read from the library's buffer; the byte count read is computed from the checked values only.
- **Addresses** added by hand are IPv4 literals in a private range, at most 16, and are stored exactly as checked. The library is never given a host name or a string with a comma.
- **The library file** can only be put in place by root on the box, and the helper can only be set up by root on the box, with the same command.

What is NOT covered: anyone on the show network can announce a sender, with any name, including the name of a real one. Keep the show network private, as for streams and OSC.

## Pads that hold an NDI source (2026-10-11; never run on a box)

The owner's words: "i want pads to hold NDI sources." A pad has a third kind beside Clip and Shader.

- **What a pad stores.** `{"label", "file": "", "ndi": "<id>"}`: the source's id and nothing else of it. No name, no address, nothing typed. No schema change. A pad with a clip is a clip pad and one with a shader is a shader pad whatever else a hand-edited file put beside them.
- **The allowed list.** `"ndi": {"sources": [{"id", "name", "from"}]}`, beside `addresses`, at most 32, read with defaults. Each row must agree with itself: the id is the one its name and machine give. A pad's id is looked up there at every press.
- **Who may allow a source.** An Operator, by putting a source that the helper lists NOW on a pad. Reason: he may already play that source by hand, and he edits pads (D80); what the list adds is that the choice lasts. A source that is away cannot be allowed, and no route takes a name or an address for it. Only the Owner removes one (the NDI page, "Sources that pads may hold", or `forget`), and only the Owner manages the addresses, the groups and the discovery servers. A pad whose source was removed keeps its id and says, when pressed, that the source is not allowed any more.
- **What a pad follows: a name at an IP address, not a sender.** The id is `ndi.source_id(name, host)`: the name the sender announces and the address the library reports for it, without the port. "A machine" below means that: whatever answers at that IP address. So a sender of the same name on ANOTHER machine is another source: the pad refuses it and says where the name is announced from now. That is stricter than "follows a name", and it has a price: a sender whose address changes (DHCP) has to be chosen for the pad again. It is still not proof of who is sending. Names are announced over mDNS with no authentication, and an address on a LAN can change hands or be taken (a new lease, ARP spoofing). Whoever holds that address and announces that name is what the pad plays. On a network you do not control, give the sender's address under "Addresses to ask" (the box then asks that machine directly and the page marks the source as "an address the owner gave") and keep that machine's address fixed; that removes the announcement from the path, not the question of who holds the address.
- **At a press**, before anything is opened or claimed: the module on, the source allowed, the helper running with its library, the source on the list now with the same name and machine as the allowed row (the id is only 48 bits of a hash, so both are compared as well). Then the one road an NDI source has, `Api.play_ndi`. A source that is away answers at once ("NDI source 'X' is not on the network now"), where opening it would have waited up to 14 seconds. **Two senders under one id** (one name twice at one address, on another port each) are refused: "the box does not pick one"; the helper marks such a row (`twins`) and such a source cannot be put on a pad. Play on the NDI page still shows such a source by hand (the helper takes the row with the lower address and port, always the same one). A press does not fall back to the row whose address and port equal the allowed row's: the port is not part of what a pad follows (it changes when a sender starts again), the helper opens a source by its id and not by a port, and "the same port as last time" is no better evidence of which sender was meant than the name is. Decided so on 2026-10-11 after the review of 8cfde96; a sender that restarts may be listed twice for a few seconds, and the pad refuses for as long.
- **A crowd cannot push a pad's source out.** The helper's list is cut to 64 rows. The panel tells the helper the ids of the allowed sources (ids only), and those are kept in the list while they are on the network, with the source on the screen, before senders at the Owner's addresses and before everything else (the review of 8cfde96: before, a few hundred announced names made every pad say "not on the network now").
- **What a Guest is told of a refusal: nothing.** The reason a pad's source was refused can name an address; the box's status does not carry it to a Guest.
- **Roles.** A Guest is refused an NDI pad as he is refused a stream: in the gate (`policy._play`), in a Room scene whose box step is such a pad (`policy._scene`), and in the handler itself; the pads' answer gives a Guest no addresses and no list of sources (he sees the pad's label as he sees any pad's, and the editor fills the label with the source's name unless the operator changes it), and his panel shows the pad as not available and never sends the tap. The allowed list, the groups and the discovery servers are in the settings export and the diagnostics file as the addresses always were: both are the Owner's routes. An Operator and the Owner play and edit.
- **Controllers.** MIDI, OSC (`/pvj/pad/<bank>/<n>`) and DMX press the pad as they press any pad (`policy.LEGACY_LIVE` is unchanged). Their press is answered at once ("pending") and the source connects on a thread of its own, through `play_ndi`; a refusal is kept for the pads (`player.ndi_refused` in the status, the line under the pads) as a shader pad's is. A Room scene and the schedule (which runs scenes) wait for the source and report it in the scene's own report. A remote support session: view is refused, live plays it as a controller would, full is the Owner. Autostart does not take an NDI pad (a sender may not be up when the box starts).
- **Lights.** The playing pad is lit by the source's id (`player.ndi_id`), on the panel, on a MIDI controller and in the state sent over OSC (`boxstate.snapshot`: `pad_ndi`, `ndi`).
- **Sound** follows the page's one switch. A pad has no sound setting.
- **An older release** (master) loads the file: the pad's `file` is empty, so it is an empty pad there ("pad is empty"), and the `ndi` key and the allowed list are kept through a load and a save. `tests/test_ndi_pads.py` runs master's own code from git for this.

## Groups and discovery servers (2026-10-11; never run on a box)

The owner's words: "make sure to incorporate the NDI group and discovery policies. i can lock the NDI network with a group policy."

**What they are.** NDI's own ways of scoping who finds whom. A sender is in one or more groups (the default is "Public"); a finder looks only in the groups it is told. A Discovery Server is a program on one machine that senders register with and finders ask.

**What they are not.** They decide what this box LOOKS for, and keep the list short and the network in order. They are not a password and they encrypt nothing: a sender that puts itself in the same group, or registers with the same discovery server, is found. So a group policy locks the network as an agreement among machines that cooperate. The pad rule about names and addresses above holds with them as without.

**What is built.**

- `"ndi": {"groups": ["Leyline"]}`: at most 8 names of 1 to 64 letters, digits, spaces, dashes, dots and underscores (no comma: the library takes them joined by commas). Kept exactly as typed: the SDK's documentation does not say whether names fold case, so this box never changes one, and the page says to type the name as the sending program has it. They go straight into the finder: `NDIlib_find_create_t.p_groups` (NULL when the list is empty, which the SDK says means the default groups). A change opens the finder again; a source on the screen plays on, since a receiver is connected by address.
- `"ndi": {"discovery": ["192.168.1.5:5959"]}`: at most 4 private or link-local IPv4 addresses, each with `:port` only when typed (the server's default is 5959). The finder's structure has no field for these. NDI reads them from its configuration file, `ndi-config.v1.json`, key `ndi.networks.discovery` (one text, comma separated), in the folder `NDI_CONFIG_DIR` names. So: root makes `/run/pvj-ndi-config` (0750 pvj-web:pvj-ndi, one more tmpfiles line), the panel writes that one file there with that one key, and the helper's unit points `NDI_CONFIG_DIR` at it. The helper can read the folder and cannot write it. The library reads the file once, when it starts, so a change makes the helper end and start again: a source on the screen stops then and is started again by hand.
- A box set up for NDI before this needs its set-up written again (an update does it; `sudo pvj-ndi-runtime refresh`; then a restart, which makes the folder). Until then the page says so and shows that the servers asked for are not in force.
- The page shows what is in force now (the groups the helper says it looks in, the servers the library was started with) beside what was asked, and says "No senders found in the group X" as a different thing from "NDI is not set up".
- A pad's source is looked for in the same list as every other source, so only within the groups and discovery in force.

**Not built, and why.**

- **A switch for mDNS.** Asked for ("mDNS on or off"). The SDK documentation (6.3.2) names no setting that stops a finder using mDNS: the `multicast`, `tcp`, `unicast` and `rudp` keys of the configuration file choose how pictures travel, not how senders are found. It also says a finder with a discovery server uses BOTH the server and mDNS; it is the SENDERS that stop announcing by mDNS once they have a discovery server. So the narrowing a venue can really have is on the sending side: give every sender the discovery server (and a group), and they are no longer announced on the local network at all. `ndi.mdns` is accepted in a settings file and read by nothing. To confirm on the Pi: step N24.
- **The group of a found source.** `NDIlib_source_t` has a name and an address and nothing else, so an allowed source does not remember a group. (The SDK's Sender Listener reports groups, and needs a discovery server; not used.)
- **Whether the file's values add to or replace what the finder is given** is not stated in the documentation. This box gives the groups and the extra addresses only through the finder and the discovery servers only through the file, so the question does not arise for them.

**Where a discovery server runs.** On a machine on the venue's network that is always on and has a fixed address: a small computer in the rack, not a laptop that leaves. Since D85 that machine can be this box: see "The discovery server on this box" below.

**Checked against the SDK (6.3.2, the owner's copy, read only; nothing of it is in this repository).** `NDIlib_find_create_t` is `show_local_sources`, `p_groups`, `p_extra_ips`, in that order, as `_FindCreate` has it. `NDIlib_source_t`, `NDIlib_recv_create_v3_t`, `NDIlib_video_frame_v2_t`, `NDIlib_audio_frame_v3_t` and the performance structure match the ctypes definitions field for field; the twelve function signatures in `_declare` match; the constants match (UYVY, UYVA, FLTP, the undefined timestamp, colour format "fastest" 100, bandwidth "highest" 100, the frame types and the field types). Nothing was wrong. Noted, not changed: the picture is read with `NDIlib_recv_capture_v2` and the sound with `_v3` on one receiver from two threads (the header allows calls at the same time and does not say whether mixing the two versions is covered); the finder's strings are kept on the library object (safe while the old finder is closed before a new one is opened, as it is).

## The discovery server on this box (D85, 2026-10-11; never run with NDI's program)

The owner's words: "let's proceed with option 2 and build the discovery server and bake into the box."

**What it is.** NDI's own program, `ndi-discovery-server`, run by the box. Senders register with it and receivers ask it. It is closed source and listens on the network. Its licence allows one copy and forbids handing it on, so it is never in this repository or an image and the box never downloads it: root places it from the owner's copy.

**What it is not.** Authentication. Anything that can reach its port can register with it and ask it who is there. It is for a network the owner controls.

**Setting it up (once per box, a keyboard or SSH).** `sudo pvj-ndi-runtime server install "<folder>"`, where the folder is the unpacked NDI SDK for Linux or NDI's Discovery Server for Linux (both keep the program at `bin/<architecture>/ndi-discovery-server`), or the file itself. It is a command of its own, not part of `pvj-ndi-runtime install`: a program that listens on the network is its own decision, and a box can have it without the NDI input. The command:

- refuses first whatever could refuse later (the unit's template, a link where a file goes, an account `pvj-ndisc` that is not what it would make);
- copies the program to `/opt/pvj-ndi-server/ndi-discovery-server`: never through a link in the last part of the name, never waiting on a pipe, at most 32 MB, and the COPY is checked before it gets its name: an ELF program (not a library, not a script) for this box's processor, root's, writable by nobody else, in a folder that is root's. Nothing is executed;
- prints the copy's sha256 to compare with the owner's file;
- makes the account and group `pvj-ndisc` (held to the rules of `pvj-ndi`: no members, no shell, no other group), writes `pvj-ndi-discovery.service` and the panel's drop-in `50-pvj-ndisc.conf` (the mark), starts the supervisor and restarts the panel.

`sudo pvj-ndi-runtime server remove` takes all of it off but the account. An update writes the unit again; an uninstall removes the set-up and, with `--purge`, the program.

**What runs.** The unit runs this project's supervisor (`bin/pvj-ndi-discovery`, `pvj/ndiscovery.py`), not NDI's program directly. The supervisor answers only the panel's account on a socket in `/run/pvj-ndisc`, and does nothing until the panel says the module is on. Then it starts NDI's program with exactly `-service -bind <address> -port <port>`, the two values checked again by the supervisor itself (a private or link-local IPv4 address or 0.0.0.0; a port from 1024 to 65535). It checks the program file before every start. If the program ends it is started again after 2 seconds, then 4, up to a minute; a run of 30 seconds resets the pause. Its last line of output is kept for the page. "Off" ends the program. `-service` is how NDI's own example systemd unit starts it (`Type=simple`, `ExecStart=... -service`; the program's help text calls it "service mode for linux/unix"). Whether the program honours `-bind` and `-port` in that mode is not known. So the supervisor reads what the program prints ("Listening... [...]"): if that names an address other than the one chosen, the program is ended and not started again, and the page says why. The page always shows the program's last line.

**The sandbox** (`pvj/systemd/pvj-ndi-discovery.service`; every line the NDI helper's unit has, and less where this needs less): its own account, no capabilities, `NoNewPrivileges`, `ProtectSystem=strict`, `ProtectHome`, a private 8 MB `/tmp`, no devices, 128 MB and one core at most; it can write only `/run/pvj-ndisc`; it cannot see the box's settings, keys, PIN, control sockets, media or the NDI helper's folders (`InaccessiblePaths`), nor the two places NDI's program would read a configuration of its own from (`/etc/ndi`, `/usr/local/etc/ndi`), so only the two arguments count; IPv4 and Unix sockets only; private and link-local addresses only, both ways, and not 127.0.0.1. It can reach the box's own LAN address, as the NDI helper can (SECURITY.md).

**A configuration of NDI's own.** NDI's program also reads `ndi-discovery-service.v1.json` from three places: `/etc/ndi`, `/usr/local/etc/ndi` and the folder the program itself lies in. The unit hides the first two. The third is `/opt/pvj-ndi-server`, root's, where this box puts the program and nothing else; if anything else lies there (that file, or any other name) the installer refuses to install over it and the supervisor refuses to start the program, and the page says which file. So the two arguments are all that count, unless root put a file there, and then nothing starts.

**The page** (Setup, NDI discovery server; the Owner's alone, and never through remote support, the module's switch included): on or off; which network (the wired one by default, the Wi-Fi, or all the box's networks, which is said to include a hotspot); the port (5959); what is listening now; "Senders and receivers on this network should use discovery server <address>:<port>" with a Copy button; and when it is not set up, the one command. No count of what is registered is shown: NDI's documentation gives no way to ask the server that without its private protocol.

**Stopping it** ends the program's whole process group: it is started in a session of its own, so whatever it may start goes with it.

**When the address changes.** The box binds the server to the chosen network's address of the moment. Every ten seconds the panel looks again; a new address (a new DHCP lease) makes the supervisor start the program again there, and the line on the page changes. Senders and receivers given the old address must be given the new one: give the box a fixed address. A network with no address (the cable pulled) stops the server; it never moves to another network by itself.

**This box's own input, and the two halves (2026-10-12).** Running a server (this page) and asking one (NDI input, the list "Discovery servers this box asks") are two halves of one set-up; the owner could not tell whether he had set them right, so each page now shows the other half. NDI input has a switch "Use this box's own discovery server", and under it the state of the server this box runs (running at its address, switched off, not set up, or switched on but not running and why) and whether the two fit; when the switch is on and the server is off, the server's own switch is offered there. This page has the same switch as "This box's own NDI input asks this server" (while NDI input is on) and the same sentence. When the server runs and the input does not ask it, both say that senders registered with it are found by this box only if they also announce themselves on the local network. NDI input also says that a sender can be found with no discovery server at all, so finding one does not prove a server is in use. NDI input reads the server's state from `GET /api/ndi/discovery`; through remote support that is refused, and the line then knows only on or off. Checked for syntax only; not run in a browser or on a box. It is kept as a switch, not as an address: the box's own address of the moment is added to what its finder asks while the server is on. ## Discovery and the network

- The library finds senders by mDNS through `avahi-daemon` (it talks to the daemon over the system D-Bus socket; the sandbox allows Unix sockets for that). mDNS is UDP port 5353, multicast, and does not cross routers or many Wi-Fi access points.
- Where mDNS does not pass, add the sender's IPv4 address on the page. The library then asks that machine directly (TCP 5960) for its sources.
- Video arrives on TCP or UDP ports from 5960 upward (5961 and up per stream, 6960 and 7960 and up for the multi-connection modes). The box runs no firewall of its own, so nothing needs opening on it. A managed switch or a firewall between sender and box must let these through.
- The helper only receives. It never announces a source.
- **A box that did not opt in shows nothing of NDI on the network**: there is no helper to ask for senders or to receive from one, and the installer adds no avahi for it. (This replaces the earlier design, in which the helper was installed idle on every box and the installer added `avahi-daemon` where it was missing.) Raspberry Pi OS has `avahi-daemon` anyway, and a box with it answers to its name by mDNS; that is the same with and without this project. On a box that is set up, the helper is idle (no library loaded, nothing sent) until the module is switched on. Device step N17 checks both on the wire.

## What the tests prove, and what they do not (2026-10-12; first run in CI the same day, on #128)

On 2026-10-12 the owner's Pi showed that no NDI picture could reach the screen of any box: the pipe had become root's and the helper still wrote only a pipe of its own. Every test was green, because every test ran the helper where it makes its own pipe. Two tests were added so that this cannot pass again. Both use a **stand-in** for NDI's library, never NDI's own.

**The stand-in** (`tests/ndi_standin/standin.c`) is our own code, written from what `pvj/ndi.py` calls (`CtypesLibrary` and the structures above it) and from nothing of NDI's. It touches no network. Its finder reports its sources (twenty-seven on 2026-10-12; the list is `SOURCES` in the file) at the private address 10.255.255.1, and words in the name of the one a receiver is opened with decide what it sends: `PVJ-STANDIN (moving)` is a 320 x 180 UYVY picture at 30 frames a second with padded lines and no sound; `PVJ-STANDIN (moving with sound)` adds 48 kHz float sound in two channels; `PVJ-STANDIN (one still then sound)` sends one picture, then only sound, which is what NDI's Test Patterns does; `PVJ-STANDIN (at once A)` and `(at once B)` have their first picture the moment they are opened (the others send nothing in their first second, as a real connection takes a moment); `PVJ-STANDIN (changes size)` goes from 320 x 180 to 256 x 144 after four seconds and stays there, as Scan Converter changes at every clip. It says what it is in its version text, so the page and the logs can never take it for NDI.

**`tests/test_ndi_standin.py`** (every machine with a C compiler; skipped without one) builds it and drives it through the helper's real code: every structure's size and field places are compared with ctypes, the pictures are read from a real pipe and compared byte for byte, the Matroska stream begins with the header `mkv_header` writes and holds the sound, and the still source gives exactly one picture while its sound goes on.

**`tests/real_install_test.sh`** (CI's job `real-install`: root, a real systemd, an Ubuntu runner that is thrown away) takes the whole road as a box has it. The stand-in is placed by the real `pvj-ndi-runtime install`, loaded by the real `pvj-ndi` service under its unit's sandbox, and asked for through the panel's API by a paired device (`tests/ndi_standin/box_check.py`). It then asserts that the helper says `playing` and counts pictures shown, that the helper's and the player's processes both hold root's pipe open, that `GET /api/status` names the source, and that mpv has the pipe loaded, is not idle, decodes 320 x 180 UYVY and moves its clock. It does this with sound off (bare frames) and with sound on (Matroska), restarts the helper under a playing source and waits for the panel to bring the source back by itself, and takes everything off again with `pvj-ndi-runtime remove`. With the helper's old check of the pipe the first of these steps fails with: `'PVJ-STANDIN (moving)' did not play: it stopped with "the input pipe was replaced; not writing to it"`.

**The helper's counts are not proof that the player plays** (2026-10-12, measured on the owner's Pi through mpv's control socket). With Test Patterns and sound on, the helper said "still" and wrote sound steadily while mpv had the pipe as its path with no track and no clock, for minutes, and the page said all was well. The same source played again with sound off was gone eight seconds later, with `playing` null and `ended` null. A source that changed size was opened again and lost the same way. So every "it plays" in the real-install test is asked of mpv itself: the pipe is its path, `track-list/count` is at least 1, there is a picture track of the stand-in's size, and `time-pos` (or `audio-pts`) moves.

**Six hand-overs, each its own step**, each from a clean start (a new helper, a new player): (1) one still picture and then only sound, with sound on; (2) the same source played twice in a row; (3) source A and then source B at once; (4) sound switched on and off again under a playing source; (5) a source that changes size while it plays; (6) the helper restarted under a playing source. After each, mpv is asked every second for ten seconds and judged at the END of that time. Good: mpv plays, or `GET /api/ndi` gives a reason in `ended`. Bad, and named in the line that begins `REASON`: `SILENT LOSS` (the page says neither) or `STUCK` (the page says it plays and mpv does not), with mpv's words second by second.

**After the second run (2026-10-12): only the first failed on the runner, while the Pi lost re-opens too.** What the Pi's cases had and the runner's had not: sound on at least one side (Matroska), pictures of megabytes against a pipe of 1 MiB (so the writer is in the middle of a picture when the old reader goes), and no clean start. So the stand-in has sources at 1280 x 720 (1.8 MB a picture; the word "big" in the name), and there are more steps: (2b) from the stuck state of the first, nothing cleaned, the same source with sound off and then sound switched on; (3b) A with sound then B with sound at once; (4b) the sound switch under a big source in both directions; (5b) a change of size with sound; and a chain of eight hand-overs back to back with nothing cleaned between them, each link judged. Steps 3 and 5 use the big sources too. Not run in CI yet.

**One switch, `NDI_FINDINGS_FATAL` at the top of that block, is "no" until the hand-over of the pipe is mended** (branch `ndi-pipe-reuse`). While it is "no", a hand-over that fails prints a line beginning `FINDING`, the run stays green and its last line lists every finding. Set to "yes", the first one fails the run. The first run in CI (#128, before these six steps existed) already showed the first of them on the runner: one still picture and then only sound left mpv with `core-idle` true and `playback-time` 0.0 for ten seconds while the helper wrote 295 blocks of sound.

**The same mpv as the Pi (2026-10-12; not run in CI yet).** A mended hand-over was green in the real-install job, whose mpv is Ubuntu's 0.37, and still stuck on the owner's Pi, whose mpv is Debian 13's 0.40: the pipe was mpv's path, it had no track and its core sat idle. A green run on another mpv proved nothing for the box. So CI has a job of its own, `ndi-mpv (Debian 13, the Pi's mpv)`, in a `debian:trixie` container. It fails if the mpv apt gives there is not 0.40.x, so that a drift is seen, and the real-install job prints its own mpv's version. It runs `tests/test_ndi_mpv.py`: the stand-in loaded by the helper's real code, a real pipe, the panel's real Api with its once-a-second watch, and a real mpv started by the real `Player` (its own command line and its own loading of the pipe; only `--vo=null --ao=null` are added). There is no systemd in it; the accounts and root's pipe stay in the real-install job. Its cases are judged by the same code (`tests/ndi_standin/mpvword.py`, which `box_check.py` uses too): a moving source with sound off and with sound, a 1280 x 720 source, one still picture and then only sound watched for 35 seconds, a moving source with sound whose picture stops, and the hand-overs (the same source twice, A then B with sound, the sound switch in both directions, a change of size with sound, the chain). The cases known to fail are findings by the same switch (`PVJ_NDI_FINDINGS_FATAL`), shown as skipped with their reason; the others are fatal. It is an x86_64 container: the same release of mpv, not the Pi's processor, GPU or sound.

**What the first run on the Pi's mpv showed, and what the Pi showed that it could not (2026-10-12).** On mpv 0.40 with master's pvj: the still source in the chain was the Pi's own picture of the fault (the pipe as mpv's path, `track-list/count` 0); a moving source whose picture stopped froze with its sound (`audio-pts` stood at 4.88 for 30 seconds); A then B with sound was lost without a word; the others passed. The still case by itself had failed for the test's own reason (it never switched the sound on), which is mended. And the Pi showed a third fault no test saw: mpv did not START the sound, because it waits until the picture reaches the first sound's time, and a real sender's first sound comes after its first picture (11 ms, or 4.7 s after a switch of the sound), while the stand-in's began at the picture's own time. So the stand-in has sources whose sound shows itself and then pauses until 0.8 s or 6 s after the first picture ("after a short gap", "after a long gap"), and wherever sound is expected the judge asks for `audio-pts` itself going on at nine tenths of the wall clock over the whole look: at 30 seconds it must be near 30, and `time-pos` alone is never taken. The job runs the still cases a second time on the pvj code of master at 5f62763, from before the mending, where each must fail by the judge's own word (STUCK, SILENT LOSS or NOT BACK); a case that passes there fails the job. Not run in CI yet in this form.

**The switches are fatal since the mending (2026-10-12), on the mending's word and not yet on a run's.** `NDI_FINDINGS_FATAL` in `tests/real_install_test.sh` defaults to "yes" and the ndi-mpv job sets `PVJ_NDI_FINDINGS_FATAL=yes`. Hand-overs that passed before the mending are asked with `ndi_must` (`must` in the test) and never were under the switch. The helper-restart step had judged wrongly on the mended code: it took "...; trying again" for an end, took the helper's answer from before its restart for the come-back ("playing again 0.0 seconds"), and compared counts of pictures across the re-open. It now waits through "trying again" up to its stated 20 seconds, believes "back" only after the source was seen gone or its count seen to start again, and prints how long that took.

**Sound against picture on a moving source (2026-10-12; not run in CI yet).** The ndi-mpv job judges it on mpv 0.40, with bounds measured there: sampled every half second from one second after playback starts, leaving out samples where the picture's clock stands, `avsync` stays within 0.040, `total-avsync-change` grows by less than 0.02 over the case (the stream as it was before the sound track began at 0 gave 0.206), `audio-pts` and `time-pos` stay within 0.1 of each other, and fewer than 1 frame in 100 is dropped. The cases: a moving source with sound; its first sound late by 0.2 s, 0.8 s and 5 s; after a re-open at another size; and a clip that is paused (five seconds moving, twenty with no picture, moving again), where the sound must have played throughout and `avsync` must be inside the bound from half a second after the picture moves again. The worst `avsync`, the growth of `total-avsync-change` and the drops are printed for every case, also when it passes. The real-install job prints the same numbers for its moving source with sound and does not judge them (another mpv).

**What that proves and what it cannot.** `avsync` is mpv's own belief about its own output: the difference between where it thinks the picture and the sound are, by the times in the stream. The helper stamps picture and sound as they ARRIVE, never with the sender's clock, so if a sender's sound and picture were out of step when they left it, mpv reads 0.000 all the same. This guards the box's own pipeline (the helper's times, the player's options, a re-open, a pause) and nothing upstream of it; and with the null outputs of CI it says nothing of a real screen's or a real sound device's delay. Whether lips match on a real display is seen by a person with a real sender.

**Sound from another sender: what the tests show (D87; 2026-10-12, written on a Mac without mpv: the unit tests ran there, the mpv cases have NOT run anywhere yet).** The stand-in has a source with sound and no picture (`PVJ-STANDIN (sound only)`, half as loud as a picture source's sound, so a test can tell whose sound is in a stream), and it now does what the library does for a receiver made with the bandwidth 10: no picture from ANY source, only sound. It counts every receiver made, by source and bandwidth, and whether it was given back (`pvj_standin_receivers`, read through ctypes in the same process): that count is how "the sound connection was opened once" is shown. Three more sound-only sources come and go by words in their names: one stops 12 s after its receiver was made (its connection is said to be down and the finder drops it), one is not reported until 5 s after the first picture's receiver, and one clicks 200 ms after each whole second of the clock while the picture source `PVJ-STANDIN (flashes)` flashes on each whole second.

- **`tests/test_ndi_standin.py`** (class `SoundFromAnotherSender`, through the helper's real `Service`, `Receiver` and `SoundFeed`): the picture of one source and the sound of the sound-only source are in one Matroska stream, the pictures byte for byte and the sound at the sound-only source's level and not the other; the sound connection is opened once and is the same object and the same receiver after the picture was opened again at another size; a sound source that appears later ends the bare picture as "changed" within the helper's two seconds and the stand-in's one; one that stops is "away" and the picture goes on with silence written; and the 200 ms between flash and click are in the stream's own times, within 60 ms.
- **`tests/test_ndi_mpv.py`, cases 40 to 47** (the ndi-mpv job, mpv 0.40, judged by mpv's own word): (a) picture and sound from two sources: two tracks, the sync bounds above, `audio-pts` at real time for 30 s, one receiver for sound alone by the stand-in's count; (b) the picture changes size: sound back within the time the re-open case without a sound source is given, one receiver for sound alone and at least two for the picture; (c) the sound source comes 5 s after the press: the press is answered within 4 s, the picture plays without sound, and mpv plays the sound within 12 s of the source appearing; (d) the sound source stops: `time-pos` goes on at real time for 12 s and never stands for a second, the page says `away` for the sound source and `playing` for the picture; (e) the 200 ms pair plays inside the sync bounds (see below), and `ndi.sound_delay_ms` is mpv's `audio-delay` for the NDI load, follows a change made while the source plays, and is 0 again on the next clip (a clip of the test's own, played by the panel); (f) Play on the sound-only source is refused with HTTP 409 in words that name it and say it sends sound and no picture, mpv has not got the pipe, and the source's row then says `sound_only`.
- **`tests/real_install_test.sh`** asks (a), (b) and (f) of the installed box, over HTTP. There the stand-in's count cannot be read (it is in the helper's process), so (b) counts the helper's own line "sound comes from" in the journal for that one process: once.

**Nothing of it is under the findings switch.** Cases 40 to 46 are fatal; when they were first written the delay was not built and its case was a finding, and it became fatal with the delay (24c2b19). Case 47 (Play on the sound-only source while a picture plays with its sound) is for the record and never fatal: it prints what became of the picture, since the panel has the player let go of the pipe before the helper answers.

**What these cases cannot show.**

- **Two real senders share no clock; the stand-in's two share one.** Its flash and its click are counted from the same clock in the same process, so their 200 ms are exact and stay exact. A real picture sender and a real sound sender each have a clock of their own, their paths through the network differ, and the distance between them may drift over an evening. No test here has two clocks. What the delay has to be on a box, and whether it holds, is found by ear with the real senders.
- **mpv's `avsync` is its own belief, and it does not see the offset.** Case 44 passes with sound that is 200 ms late by construction: the helper stamps picture and sound as they arrive, the stream says they belong together, and mpv reads about 0.000. That case is there to say so on every run. In sync by mpv's word means the box's own pipeline added nothing; it never means the sound meets the picture.
- **`audio-delay` is a property, not heard sound.** Case 45 shows that the player was told the number (positive makes the sound later, so sound that is late is put right with a negative delay), and that it is taken back. With the null outputs of CI nothing shows that flash and click then meet.
- **"Away" is the stand-in's kind of stopping.** It says the connection is down (the library's error frame) and leaves the finder. Whether NDI's library says that for a sound-only connection whose sender quit, or only goes quiet (then the page says `playing` with no sound arriving), is not known from this; neither is whether it reconnects by itself.
- **The level, the channels, and a sender that connects and sends nothing.** The stand-in's sound is two channels at 48 kHz in blocks of a thirtieth of a second. The owner's sender sent six channels in blocks of 800 samples on one day and nothing on another (see "Tried on the Pi's network").
- **Bandwidth 10 is taken on the helper's word.** The stand-in treats 10 as "sound alone" because `pvj/ndi.py` says so; that NDI's library does is in "Checked against the SDK and the real library", by hand, once.

What neither test shows:

- **That NDI's own library works**, loads on a Pi, finds a sender, or gets through the sandbox's network rules (`IPAddressAllow`, the system bus to avahi). The stand-in does none of that. Only a box with the owner's copy of the library and a real sender can show it.
- **That a picture is on a screen.** The runner has no display, and without any output mpv plays nothing and lets go of the pipe. So for these steps only, the test starts the player with mpv's null video and sound outputs (a drop-in of its own in `/run`, removed at the end). What is shown is that the player reads and decodes the pipe; what a DRM screen does with one still frame can only be seen on a box.
- **That the file `pvj-ndi-runtime install` places is NDI's.** The command checks that it is a plain file for this processor and not too large, and accepts the stand-in as it accepts any library. The sha256 it prints is the owner's only check of whose file it is.
- **Anything about a Raspberry Pi.** The runner is x86_64 with Ubuntu's systemd and Ubuntu's mpv; the box has mpv 0.35, where the pipe's options may be set another way (`Player._play_pipe` has a road for each).

## Not verified

- **Pads, groups and discovery servers, on any box.** Written and tested against the faked library on a Mac. Never run with a real sender: steps N22 to N26.

- **The real command succeeding, anywhere.** `pvj-ndi-runtime install` has only ever been run to the end with stand-ins: it needs NDI's library. CI runs it with a file it refuses, and calls the setup it would go on to directly.
- **The opt-in on a device.** `pvj-ndi-runtime install` with a real SDK on a Pi: that `apt-get`, the account, the unit and the drop-ins come out as written, that the panel and the player come back with the extra group, that an update keeps it and `remove` takes it off (steps N2, N17 and N18). `tests/test_ndi_setup.py` drives it with a fake root folder and fake commands; `tests/real_install_test.sh` runs its root part under the CI runner's systemd without a library. Neither is a Pi.
- **The real library on Linux, beyond one run.** On 2026-10-08, once, on a Pi 4: `libndi.so.6` loaded under the sandbox, discovery worked from inside the unit, and progressive UYVY frames of two senders were received and played ("First run on a Pi 4"). Not shown: other senders and sizes, 60 frames a second, HX, alpha, a long run, or anything by eye.
- **Why a snapshot of a still source fails** while its picture is on the screen (seen once, through a camera pointed at the monitor): step N12. And whether a still source is drawn as well when the stream carries sound (the Matroska path), which has not been on a box.
- **An interlaced sender**: which frame types really arrive and that they are refused as written (step N13).
- **The bounds in the unit** (memory, tasks, processor, the small `/tmp`), and discovery without 127.0.0.1 (step N14). The helper can reach the box's own LAN address (it lies inside the allowed private ranges): the trust boundary is in SECURITY.md, and N21 is the thing to try against it.
- **Sound, on any box.** Nobody has heard it. Unknown: the delay; whether sound and picture are in step and stay so over minutes; what a correction of the count sounds like (40 ms or more of sound cut, or of silence put in); what mpv does where silence was filled in; the level of a film against a clip; the cost on a Pi 4; whether the player's queue is ever reached at 1080p; mpv 0.35 and 0.40 with the narrowing (CI's is 0.37).
- **The player and the one pipe** (2026-10-12). Everything about the order of readers was tested with threads standing in for mpv. Not seen with a real mpv: that `stop` makes mpv close the pipe and say `idle-active` within the 1.5 seconds the panel waits and the 2 seconds the helper waits (the page and the helper's log say so when not), also for a load that is stuck opening; and that nothing else in mpv opens the pipe between the helper's look and the panel's load. That a pipe nobody holds is empty is how Linux and macOS pipes work; it was run on macOS only. To try on the Pi: pads that switch between two NDI sources of different sizes, quickly, twenty times; the same with the first source paused; a pad pressed twice; and `journalctl -u pvj-ndi` for "still held the pipe".
- **A still source with sound: mpv paused "for buffering" for as long as the picture stood, and before that mpv 0.40 never finished opening the stream. Measured on the Pi's mpv 0.40 run by hand; NOT yet seen on the box's own player and screen (2026-10-12).** What was seen on the box: Test Patterns (one picture, then only its tone) with sound on, the helper "still", shown 1, sound written ten times a second, mpv with the pipe as its path and no tracks. The experiment (the helper's own writer functions feeding a pipe at real time, 1920 x 1080 UYVY and 100 ms blocks of float sound; mpv 0.40 as `pvj-dev` with `--vo=null --ao=null` and every option the box sets for this load; asked over mpv's own socket) found two separate faults. (1) With ONE picture block mpv stays in the open: `track-list/count` 0 until the pipe ends, and its log says "Opening done" only then. That is `probe_if_image` (`demux/demux_mkv.c`, in v0.38.0 to v0.40.0, not in v0.35.0 or v0.37.0): it reads on until the picture track has had two blocks with DIFFERENT times, or 10000 blocks. The first cure (the first picture written twice) did nothing on the box because both copies had the time 0: the first picture always arrives before the pipe is taken, its time was below 0 and was raised to 0, and so was the copy's. No test had a first picture older than the stream. (2) With two picture blocks mpv opens at once (2 tracks) and then: `Enter buffering (buffer went from 100% -> 0%)`, `paused-for-cache` true, `core-idle` true, time 0, no sound, until the pipe ends (`End buffering (waited 14.799834 secs)`). mpv takes a picture track that has run dry for a slow network and pauses everything until a picture comes (`player/playloop.c`, `handle_update_cache`; the same code at v0.37.0, which is what CI's runner showed). The same happens to a moving source that goes still: 10 seconds of picture, then still, and the sound stopped with it; when the picture moved again 12 seconds later, mpv played the sound it had kept, 12 seconds late, and stayed that late. What was changed: the player loads the stream with `cache-pause=no` (`Player._play_pipe`, taken back with the other options), and the helper writes a picture that no other has followed for half a second once more (`STILL_AGAIN_SECONDS`; the stream's first picture at once, a millisecond later, and never at the same time). The second is needed besides the first because mpv shows a picture only when it has the one after it: without it the last picture before a still was never drawn (mpv's `time-pos` stood one picture back). What was measured with both: one still and sound for 135 seconds, 2 tracks from the start, `audio-pts` 134.6 at 135.0 seconds, never `paused-for-cache`, no "Too many packets"; and 10 seconds of picture, 130 of still, 10 of picture again: sound all the way, and the picture back in step with the sound within a second of moving (`time-pos` 149.2, `audio-pts` 149.6). The other way was measured too, with mpv's pause left on: the still written again every second, and every quarter second (40 seconds each). Both play, sound and all; mpv then runs behind by about the gap (sound 1.4 seconds behind the pipe with a repeat a second, 0.7 with four a second, 0.4 without any of this) after pausing "for buffering" four or five times at the start, and a 1080p picture is 4 MB each time, for as long as it stands. So the pause is switched off instead, and a picture that stands costs one more picture, once. Also run there: the four tests of `tests/test_ndi_sound.py` that need a real mpv (`RealMpvTest`), with the real helper code, on mpv 0.40: they pass. What this does not show: the box's own player, its screen and its sound device (the experiment had neither), and mpv 0.37 (CI's end-to-end test is the check: the still hand-over in `tests/real_install_test.sh`). While the picture stands mpv's `time-pos` stands with it and `audio-pts` goes on: anything that judges "is it playing" must look at both.
- **One frame, then "a still picture" for 90 seconds, on the first play on the Pi (2026-10-12, 18:43 UTC).** Read from the counters, not seen: "received 0" is counted from the moment the player took the pipe, so the library handed the helper no second picture in those 90 seconds; and the stream was decided as bare frames, which with sound switched on means no sound came in the first half second, and none came later either, because sound that starts later ends the source as "changed" at once. A sender that sends neither picture nor sound is a paused or silent window, as far as the helper can tell. It is not the pipe's race in another form: that ends the source ("stopped") or leaves the count of received frames climbing. What the counters cannot say is whether the one frame reached the screen: before this change it could have gone to a reader left over from the plays that failed that morning. If it happens again with the sender surely playing, the thing to read is `dropped_by_runtime` and the helper's log.
- **Everything that touches mpv**: the raw pipe in `uyvy422` (the capture input's `yuyv422` path is proven on the Pi 4; this byte order is not), a fractional frame rate (59.94), and the reload on a size change.
- **Every sandbox line** in `pvj-ndi.service`, above all `IPAddressDeny`/`IPAddressAllow` (it needs the kernel's cgroup network filter), `PrivateDevices` and `RestrictAddressFamilies`. LESSONS has two entries where a sandbox line emptied a feature silently on the Pi. Also unverified on a device: that the player and the panel start with the extra group `pvj-ndi` from their drop-ins after the opt-in, and after an update over it.
- **Speed.** Full NDI is decoded in software by the library; 1080p30 UYVY is 124 MB a second through the pipe (the capture input managed that on a Pi 4), 1080p60 is twice that and has never been tried. NDI HX is H.264 or HEVC decoded by the library in software and may be heavier still. Latency is unmeasured.
- A Pi 3 is not offered (the module lists Pi 4, Pi 5 and x86).
- The browser test step for the page was written and syntax-checked, not run (the dev Mac has no Playwright). Neither was the installer on the dev Mac: its tests (`tests/test_install.py`) need GNU tools and run in CI only, as does the real install under systemd (`tests/real_install_test.sh`).
- Health does not list the NDI helper; the NDI page and its row on System say when it is not running.

## Open design questions

- **Sound: decided and built on 2026-10-08**, option (b) of the three that were weighed here (a second pipe of raw samples; one stream that holds both with times; leaving sound to the room): one stream with times, as Matroska and not NUT because mpv reads Matroska itself and the writer is forty lines. See "Sound". Open: the level of program senders, lip sync and delay on a box, and whether the first two channels are the right ones for the senders the owner uses.
- **Low bandwidth.** The library can ask a sender for its small proxy stream; a "small picture" choice per source would make a Pi 3 or a weak Wi-Fi link usable. Not built.
- **A source as a pad, in a scene or in the schedule.** The id is a hash of the name, so it stays the same across restarts and could be saved. Not built.
- **Getting the library onto a box without a keyboard.** A USB stick route (root's USB unit copies `ndi/libndi.so.6` from a stick) would suit a venue; physical access is already the box. Not built; needs the licence read first.

## Alternatives that were rejected

- **mpv or ffmpeg with NDI built in.** Debian's and Raspberry Pi OS's ffmpeg have no NDI (as far as is remembered, ffmpeg removed its NDI device in 2019 after a licence dispute; this was not looked up for this work), so it would mean shipping our own ffmpeg and mpv linked against a proprietary library.
- **The library inside the player or the panel.** A crash would take the screen down (the reason the capture input has a helper), and a takeover would have the PIN, the settings and the stream passwords (panel) or the screen and the control socket (player).
- **The helper as a child of the panel**, like the capture helper. It would inherit the panel's account and its access to the settings.
- **A compiled helper** (C, with or without GStreamer's NDI plugin). Faster to a known-good result, but it breaks "standard library only" and adds a build per board. If the Pi 4 measurement shows Python's copy is the bottleneck, this is the next thing to weigh, and it is the owner's call.
- **YUV4MPEG instead of raw frames.** It carries the size in a header, but only planar formats, so every frame would be rearranged in Python.
- **An upload route for the library.** See above.
