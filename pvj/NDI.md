# NDI® input (beta; the picture has had one first run on a Pi 4; nobody has heard its sound yet)

Show the picture of Resolume, MadMapper, OBS or any other NDI sender on the box's screen, like a clip. **With its sound** since 2026-10-08, when the owner said "go ahead with sound": the sound a source sends is played with its picture through the box's own sound output, volume and mute, and one switch on the page turns it off. **Nobody has heard it yet** (see "Sound"). **It is opt-in per box:** a box that was only installed has nothing of NDI on it and nothing of it runs; one root command at the box sets it up (see "Setting NDI up on a box"). Then switch it on under System > NDI input. Information about NDI and its tools is at [ndi.video](https://ndi.video/); the free tools (Test Patterns, Screen Capture, Studio Monitor) are at [ndi.video/tools](https://ndi.video/tools/).

NDI® is a registered trademark of Vizrt NDI AB.

**State on 2026-10-08.** The receiver, its service, the routes and the page are written and tested against a fake library and fake frames. The library layer met the real NDI library on a Mac on 2026-10-07 (see "Measured against the real library"). **On 2026-10-08 it ran for the first time on a Pi 4** (the owner's test Pi, Debian 13, mpv 0.40): the opt-in command worked, the library loaded inside the sandbox, senders were found, and a moving source was received and played by mpv for a minute with no frame dropped by the helper (see "First run on a Pi 4"). **That was one short run, from a sender on Wi-Fi, by SSH**; the colours and geometry were judged from one preview picture, and a still source did not give a snapshot. The owner then looked at the moving source on the Pi's monitor and said "The pi looked good": that is the first look by eye, at a moving source only; the still source has not been looked at. **Sound was built after that run and has not been on the box: nobody has heard it.** Not measured at all: the sound on a box, lip sync, latency, 60 frames a second, 1080p60, NDI HX, an interlaced sender, a run of five minutes or more, and everything under "Not verified". **The licence:** the owner has decided to accept the NDI SDK licence himself, for testing on his own test Pi only. **Whether a venue box may use the library is still open, and is the owner's question to settle with NDI** (see "The SDK License Agreement itself").

## Sound (built 2026-10-08; measured on the dev Mac; nobody has heard it)

The owner said "go ahead with sound" on 2026-10-08. What is here was built and tested on the dev Mac, against fakes and against the real senders of NDI Tools through the real library, with nothing played aloud. **What a box plays, how late, whether lips and voice agree, and how loud it is in the room are device step N10.**

**What it does.** For the source on the screen the helper also takes the source's sound from the library, and the one mpv plays it with the picture on the output the box already uses, under the box's own volume, mute and choice of sound device (the Sound page). The helper opens no sound device and its sandbox is unchanged.

**How the sound travels.** With sound, the pipe no longer carries bare frames. It carries a stream the helper writes itself: Matroska with uncompressed video (the same UYVY bytes, FourCC `UYVY`) and 32-bit float PCM, with a time on every frame and on every block of sound. mpv reads it with its own Matroska reader, named in the options so that it does not guess. A source that sends no sound, and every source when sound is switched off, is played exactly as before: bare frames and the raw video options, which is the path that ran on the Pi.

**Why a stream with times, and not a second pipe of raw sound.** A player given two raw pipes tells the time by counting: frame 300 is at ten seconds, sample 480000 is at ten seconds. This helper drops a frame whenever the screen is slow (the newest wins) and writes a still frame twice; each of those would move the picture by a whole frame against the sound, for good. So the helper says when each thing belongs, and the player keeps them together.

**Whose clock.** The helper's own, never the sender's. Measured against the real senders: the time the library gives a block of sound is when it was sent, and jitters (steps of 35 to 165 ms between blocks of exactly 100 ms), and Test Patterns' one still frame carried a time 8061 seconds older than its sound. So a frame belongs where it arrived, and sound is placed by counting its samples from where its first block arrived (one block's length earlier, since a block is sent when its last sample exists). The count is held within 0.3 seconds of the clock: if the sound falls behind (a gap), it starts again at the clock; if it runs ahead (a sender whose clock is fast, or a burst), a block is left out and counted. That also bounds the drift between the sender's 48 kHz and the box's.

**What happens when.**

- **No sound from the source:** after the first picture the helper waits half a second for sound; if none comes, the source is shown as bare frames. If sound starts later, the helper ends the stream as "changed" and the panel opens the source again, now with sound: the picture is gone for a second or two, once.
- **The rate or the number of channels changes:** the same "changed" and reopening, as when a picture changes size.
- **The sound stops while the picture goes on** (the sender mutes, or blocks are lost): the helper writes silence for the stretch that is missing, so the player does not hold the picture back waiting for sound. Counted as "filled".
- **The screen is slow or the player stops reading:** at most one second of sound waits; beyond that the oldest is dropped and counted. It never piles up.
- **A still picture with sound** (Test Patterns): the frame is written once more with a later time, as without sound, and the sound goes on.
- **More than two channels:** the first two are played. NDI says how many channels there are and nothing about what each one is, so there is nothing to mix down by. (The Mac's "macOS AV Output" sender sent six, with sound only in the first two.) A mono source is played as one channel.
- **Rates:** 32, 44.1, 48, 88.2 and 96 kHz are taken as they are (mpv converts for the output). Anything else is not played, and the page says so; the picture goes on.

**How loud.** NDI's sound is floating point in which 1.0 is a professional reference level (+4 dBu), not full scale; a sender may go well above 1.0. The SDK's header for its own 16-bit helpers describes a "reference level" in decibels for this and advises 20 when receiving, which puts full scale 20 dB above reference. The helper does the same, in one named constant (`AUDIO_HEADROOM_DB = 20` in `pvj/ndi.py`): every sample is multiplied by 0.1, and what would still be over full scale is held there. Measured: Test Patterns' tone is 1 kHz and peaks at exactly 1.0, so the pipe carries it peaking at -20.00 dBFS (RMS -23.01), which is where an SMPTE alignment tone belongs. **Not measured:** what level a program source such as a film through Scan Converter arrives at (Scan Converter sent no sound when it was tried). If such senders put digital full scale at 1.0 and not at 10.0, their sound will come out 20 dB quieter than a clip; step N10 compares the two by ear, and the constant is the one thing to change.

**What is refused.** Every figure of a block of sound comes from the network through closed code and is checked before a byte is read: the format (32-bit float planes only), the rate (from the list above), the channels (1 to 64 said, two read), the samples in a block (1 to 48000, never more than a second), the distance between planes (at least a block, at most a bounded padding), and the address. A block that fails is counted and left out; it never ends the picture. A block that holds a NaN, an infinity or a sample more than 60 dB over reference is played as silence of the same length. The times the sender gives are not used at all.

**The switch.** System > NDI input, "Play a source's sound": on unless someone switches it off (`"ndi": {"sound": false}` in the settings; no new schema, a file without the key means on). On by default because the owner asked for sound and a source's sound belongs with its picture as a clip's does; the box's volume and mute apply either way. Switching it off is also the way back to the picture path that was measured. Switching it while a source is on the screen reopens the source.

**What the page shows.** Whether sound is arriving, the channels and rate, the loudest sample of the last second in dB under full scale or "silent", and how many blocks were dropped, could not be read, or were played as silence.

**Measured on the dev Mac (2026-10-08), with the real library and NDI Tools, nothing played aloud.**

- **Test Patterns:** 48000 Hz, 2 channels, blocks of 4800 samples (100 ms), float planes, plane distance 19200 bytes. A 1 kHz tone (999.8 Hz by zero crossings), peak 1.00000, RMS 0.70709. Through the helper into a pipe for ten seconds: the stream was Matroska, decided 0.06 seconds after the first picture; 100 blocks received and 100 written, none dropped, each following the one before with no gap; the pipe's sound peaked at 0.10000, -20.00 dBFS. It was also written to a WAV outside the repository to look at; it was not played.
- **macOS AV Output:** 48000 Hz, 6 channels, blocks of 800 samples; sound in the first two channels only (peaks 0.33 and 0.35). It sent no picture when the helper was run against it, so it was not played through the pipe.
- **Scan Converter:** no sound in 8 seconds and none in a ten second run (shown as bare frames, decided after the half second's wait).
- **Cost:** converting ten seconds of 48 kHz stereo took 77 ms of one core on the Mac. A Pi 4 is several times slower; the figure there is not known.

**What CI shows, and what only the box can.** Where mpv is installed (CI's Linux runner; not the dev Mac) a test runs the helper's real writer into a real pipe read by the real mpv with the player's options, without a display or a sound device: mpv must take the stream from a pipe, find a 64 x 16 picture whose colour is the red that was sent (so the byte order survives the container), and write out 48 kHz stereo sound of the length that was sent with the tone at -20 dBFS. That shows mpv can read the stream. It does not show anything a person would hear, the delay, or whether sound and picture are in step on a real screen with real loudspeakers: mpv runs there as fast as it can, and the runner's mpv is not the Pi's (0.40) nor Bookworm's (0.35; its Matroska reader was read and takes the same elements, which is not the same as having been run).

## Setting NDI up on a box (opt-in: one command, and what it changes)

The owner's answer of 2026-10-08: "install the helper only when NDI is wanted; one extra command at the box, alongside the runtime install."

**A box that was only installed** (`install/install.sh`, with or without `--offline`, an update from the panel, or the image) has, of NDI, only program files and the command `pvj-ndi-runtime` on the path. It has no `pvj-ndi` service, no `pvj-ndi` account or group, no folder `/run/pvj-ndi` or `/opt/pvj-ndi`, and nothing in the units of the panel and the player; the installer adds no avahi package for it; nothing of NDI listens, asks or announces on the network. The image build does not opt in. On such a box the NDI page says "NDI is not set up on this box" and shows the command below, the address list can already be filled in, and `GET /api/ndi` answers `"setup": false` without trying any socket.

**The one command.** The NDI runtime library, `libndi.so.6`, is proprietary; it is not in this repository and it is never in the image. Somebody with the right to accept NDI's licence fetches the NDI SDK for Linux from [ndi.video](https://ndi.video/) (the download asks you to agree to its licence), and at the box, as root, runs:

    sudo pvj-ndi-runtime install "/path/to/NDI SDK for Linux"      # the unpacked SDK folder, or the libndi.so.6 file itself

**Where to unpack the SDK.** In a folder only you can write: root's home, your own home, or a USB stick you brought. **Not in the media folder** (`/var/lib/pvj/video`) or anywhere under `/var/lib/pvj`: the panel's account can write there, and root would be reading a library from a place an unprivileged service controls. The command follows a link given as the file only to a plain name in the same folder, but it cannot know who else could have put the file there.

It first looks at everything that could refuse the setup, before anything on the box is changed: the install folder the installer wrote down, the unit's template, something that is not a plain file where one of its three files goes (a link or a folder; it says which, and "nothing was changed"), and a group or an account `pvj-ndi` that is already there (below). Then it does two things, in this order, and can be run again to repair or to put a newer library in place:

1. **The library.** It picks the file for this board from the SDK, checks it (see below) and copies it to `/opt/pvj-ndi/libndi.so.6`. A file that is refused ends the command there: nothing else is changed, and the folder `/opt/pvj-ndi` is not left behind either.
2. **The helper** (`pvj/ndisetup.py`, root's code for this and nothing else):
   - installs `avahi-daemon` and `libavahi-client3` with `apt-get` if they are missing (the library finds senders through them; Raspberry Pi OS has both already). Without a network this is said and the rest goes on; senders are then not found until the packages are there;
   - makes the system group and account `pvj-ndi` (no home, no shell, in no other group). An account of that name that is already there and is not that (it is root under another name, shares its number with another account, has a login shell, or is in another group) is refused, not repaired, and so is a group `pvj-ndi` that is root's group under another name, shares its number with another group (an account made into it would be in that other group, `pvj` for one), has members, or is somebody else's own group. Members are refused because whoever is in the group can enter the helper's folder; the panel and the player are let in by their units, never by membership. All of that is found out before the library is copied or a package installed. Should the account come out of `useradd` other than asked (a box whose own rules for new accounts add a group or a shell), the command stops, says that the account was made a moment ago and how to remove it;
   - writes `/etc/systemd/system/pvj-ndi.service` (from `pvj/systemd/pvj-ndi.service`, which travels with the program) and two drop-ins, `pvj-web.service.d/50-pvj-ndi.conf` and `pvj-player.service.d/50-pvj-ndi.conf`, each adding the extra group `pvj-ndi` to that service; the panel's also sets `PVJ_NDI_DIR`, which is how the panel knows the box is set up;
   - reloads systemd, enables and starts `pvj-ndi`, looks for three seconds whether it stays up, and restarts the panel and the player if they are running, because an extra group only reaches a process when it starts. **The screen goes dark for a moment**; do it before a show, not during one.

**What it says at the end, and what a failure leaves.** "NDI is set up on this box: the helper pvj-ndi is installed and was running 3 seconds after its start", or, if the helper was started and did not stay up, "NDI is set up on this box, but the helper pvj-ndi is NOT running" with where to look; the setup is then left in place so that it can be looked at on the box, and the command ends with an error. If writing one of the three files (a full or read-only disk, something in its place), reloading systemd, enabling or starting the helper itself fails on a box that was not set up before, the unit and the drop-ins are taken away again and the command says "the setup failed and was undone: this box is not set up for NDI"; what stays then is the library, the account and group, and any package that was installed (`sudo pvj-ndi-runtime remove` takes the library off). On a box that was already set up, a failed run leaves it set up.

**On a box with the read-only root** (`sudo pvj-rootfs enable`, see `pvj/README.md`): while it is active everything written since the last start is in memory and is gone at the next restart, and that includes the library, the account and the helper's unit. The command says so when it sees it, at its start and at its end, and goes on. To set NDI up so that it stays: `sudo pvj-rootfs disable`, restart, run the command, `sudo pvj-rootfs enable`, restart. The same holds for `remove` and for any update.

**Why this command and not an installer flag.** A box made from the image, or updated from the panel, has no copy of the installer on it: only `pvj/` and `bin/` are kept. A flag on `install.sh` could not be "one command at the box" there. `pvj-ndi-runtime` is on every box's path, was already the root command for the library, and the unit it writes travels with the program.

**An update keeps an opted-in box opted in.** The panel's drop-in is the mark. (The panel itself reads `PVJ_NDI_DIR` from its environment, which that drop-in sets. Root could also set it in `/etc/pvj/pvj.env`; on a box without the helper the page would then say "the helper is not running", which is the wrong words and nothing more. The installer never writes it there.) `install.sh` (and so every update) looks for it; where it is there, the unit and the two drop-ins are written again by the newly installed program (`pvj-ndi-runtime refresh`, the same code that wrote them, so there is one writer), the helper stays enabled, and it is restarted (with `--no-start`, as an update from the panel runs it, only if it was running). Where the mark is not there, the installer does nothing about NDI, with one exception: a helper's unit, the player's drop-in or the link that enabled the unit, without the mark (a setup that was cut short, or a box that ran this branch while it still put the helper on every box; no such box is known) is disabled and removed, and the installer says "removing an NDI helper that nobody set up on this box". The account, if there is one, stays. **So a box that ran an earlier build of this branch loses its helper at the next install or update and needs the opt-in command again.** A link where the mark goes is not an opt-in either, but systemd would read a drop-in through it, so the installer removes the link (the link itself, never what it points to) and says so with a WARNING. If one of these cannot be removed because a folder is in its place, the installer says which with a WARNING and goes on; it never stops an install or an update for it.

**A rollback** (`pvj-update rollback`, or an update that fails its health check) switches the program back and writes no units. Between two releases that both have the NDI input, the updater restarts a running helper together with the panel and the player, so all three run the same release. Back to a release from before the NDI input, the helper's unit and the drop-ins stay and the helper's program is gone: the unit has `ConditionPathExists=` on its program, so it is skipped, once, and is not started again and again (without that line it would fail every two seconds into the kept journal). The command `pvj-ndi-runtime` is then a link to nothing and cannot be run. The panel and the player start as before (the group they name still exists). To take the helper off such a box by hand:

    sudo systemctl disable --now pvj-ndi.service
    sudo rm -f /etc/systemd/system/pvj-web.service.d/50-pvj-ndi.conf /etc/systemd/system/pvj-player.service.d/50-pvj-ndi.conf /etc/systemd/system/pvj-ndi.service
    sudo systemctl daemon-reload
    sudo systemctl try-restart pvj-web pvj-player

or update forward again, after which `sudo pvj-ndi-runtime remove` works. Nothing of this has been tried on a box; the CI runner tries the skipped unit with the helper's program moved away.

**Opting out, and uninstalling.**

    sudo pvj-ndi-runtime status      # says whether the library is in place and whether the box is set up
    sudo pvj-ndi-runtime remove      # opt out

`remove` stops and disables the helper, removes its unit, the two drop-ins and the library, and restarts the panel and the player without the extra group. If one of those files cannot be removed (something else is in its place), it says which, goes on with the rest and the library, and ends with an error. It leaves the account and group `pvj-ndi` (`sudo deluser pvj-ndi; sudo delgroup pvj-ndi` removes them) and the avahi packages, which may have been on the box before and which other things may use; it says so. So if the opt-in installed `avahi-daemon`, it keeps running after the opt-out until someone removes it. A box with `avahi-daemon` answers to its name by mDNS whether or not NDI is set up; that is avahi, not NDI. `install.sh --uninstall` removes the helper's unit and the drop-ins with everything else and leaves `/opt/pvj-ndi` (the owner's copy of the library), the account and the group; `--uninstall --purge` removes `/opt/pvj-ndi` too. A box installed again after an uninstall has not opted in.

**Before installing it: NDI's licence may not cover a box like this one: read it before you install the runtime. This is not legal advice.** The agreement names kinds of product its free licence does not cover, and a small box built for one job may be one of them; see "The SDK License Agreement itself" below. The owner of the box decides, and asks NDI if in doubt.

**The checks on the library file.** The command picks the file for this board from the SDK's `lib/` folder, refuses a file built for another kind of processor and says which ("this file is for x86_64, this box is aarch64"), and copies it to `/opt/pvj-ndi/libndi.so.6` (root's, read-only for every service). It needs root on the box (a keyboard or SSH), on purpose: see "Why there is no upload button". A link given as the file is followed only where the SDK itself has one: to a plain name in the same folder (`libndi.so.6` to `libndi.so.6.3.2`), at most two links deep; a link that points into another folder, to a name with a folder in it, to nothing, or on to more links is refused, and the file that is then opened is opened without following a link. It does not wait on a pipe, reads no more than the size it allows, and keeps its copy private until every check has passed.

If the library is taken away by hand on a box that is set up, the page says "The NDI runtime is not on this box yet" and shows the same command; its switch, its list and its address form are on the same page.

## How it works

    sender --NDI--> libndi.so.6 (in pvj-ndi, its own account and sandbox)
                      | UYVY frames, whole frames only, newest wins
                      v
                  /run/pvj-ndi/ndi.fifo  --read by-->  the one mpv (rawvideo demuxer, uyvy422)

- `pvj-ndi.service` (only on a box that was set up for NDI) runs `pvj/ndi.py` as the account `pvj-ndi`. It loads the library with `ctypes` (standard library; nothing is compiled), asks it for the senders on the network, and, when the panel asks for one, receives its frames.
- Frames are asked for in the library's "fastest" format, which is 8-bit UYVY (UYVY plus a separate alpha plane when the sender has alpha; the alpha plane is not read). No colour conversion happens in Python or in the library.
- The helper writes raw frames into a pipe; the player plays the pipe with mpv's raw video demuxer, as it already does for the HDMI capture input (`pvj/capture.py`, measured on the Pi 4 at 1080p30). The player never loads the library.
- The size and frame rate are not known until the first frame. So: the panel asks the helper to open a source; the helper connects and waits up to 6 seconds for a frame; it answers with the width, height and rate; the panel loads the pipe in the player with exactly those; the helper starts writing when the player opens the pipe.
- **A sender that changes size or rate**: the helper stops writing (the raw pipe has one fixed layout) and says "changed"; the panel, which looks at the helper once a second while NDI is on screen, opens the source again and reloads the pipe. The picture is gone for the second or two that takes.
- **A still picture** (a test pattern, a paused output, a slide): the sender sends a frame and then nothing new. That is normal and is left alone: the page says "On the screen, a still picture", and nothing is reloaded however long it lasts. **A frame that nothing follows within 0.3 seconds is written to the pipe once more** (counted as `repeated`, apart from `shown`). The reason is the first run on a Pi 4, where mpv held a still source's one frame at frame 0, idle, and could make no snapshot of it: mpv, as far as its source is remembered here (it was not read again and this was not tried), shows a frame only once it holds the one after it. If that is right, the repeat is what puts a still picture, and the last frame before any pause, on the screen, about a third of a second late; if it is wrong it costs one frame. **Not tried on a box: step N12.** A sender on a poor link that pauses for a third of a second gets one frame doubled.
- **A snapshot of an NDI source that the player cannot make** (Pads, "Take snapshot") is answered with 409 and "no snapshot yet: the NDI source has not given the player enough pictures to copy one", and the page shows those words; it was a 503 with mpv's own error. It says nothing about what the screen shows, because the panel cannot know.
- **A sender that goes away**: only when the library itself reports that the connection dropped does the page say "The connection to the sender dropped; waiting for it". The helper keeps the connection (the library reconnects by itself), the screen holds the last frame, and when frames return they continue; if they return in another size, see above. If the helper has nothing open (it started again, or the sender was gone at a reload), the panel tries to show the source again after 3 seconds, then 6, 12 and so on up to once a minute, and writes the reason in the log once.
- **A source is a name on a machine.** The id the panel plays by is made from the checked name and the address of the machine that announces it (not the port, which changes when a sender restarts). A second machine announcing the same name is another row and can never take the place of the one that was chosen. The price: a sender whose address changes (DHCP) becomes a new row and has to be chosen again; staying with the machine was preferred to following the name.
- **Too many senders**: at most 64 are listed. Which ones does not depend on the order they are announced in: the one on the screen first, then senders at an address the owner added, then the rest by name. The page says when the list was cut and that adding the sender's address brings it in.
- **A source that ends by itself** (a format this input does not show, or the player moved on) is no longer "what plays"; the page keeps the reason until something is played again.
- **Something else is played while a source is still connecting** (it can take seconds): the later choice stands; the source is let go and the screen is not touched. Everything that loads or clears the player through the panel's own calls cancels a connecting source first, and waits if the NDI pipe is being loaded at that very moment. Two paths load the player without going through that door (a client box following its server in "Boxes in step", and a shader swap); for those the pipe simply loses its reader and the helper lets the source go.
- **Slow screen, fast sender**: the helper holds one frame; a newer one replaces it and the old one is counted as dropped. A frame is never written in part. The counts (received, shown, dropped here, dropped by the library) are on the page and in the status route.
- **Interlaced senders are refused**, with a plain message ("the source sends interlaced video, which this input does not show; set the sender to progressive"). That covers all three interlaced frame types the library has: a frame of two fields woven together (value 0), and single fields (2 and 3). Only a progressive frame (1) is shown. Why not have the library de-interlace: as NDI's documentation of receiving describes it (in this project's words), the library de-interlaces when the receiver's `allow_video_fields` setting is false, and the "fastest" colour format always implies that setting to be true. So de-interlacing in the library means giving up the fastest format (the alternative with UYVY hands frames with alpha over as BGRA, converted), for senders that Resolume and MadMapper are not. The helper therefore asks for fastest with fields allowed, said outright, and refuses what is not progressive. **To be confirmed on a device with an interlaced sender** (step N8): which of the three values really arrive, and that the message is what the page shows.
- **Sound** is taken from the library on a thread of its own and travels in the same pipe as the picture, each with its time: see "Sound" above. With sound off, or from a source that sends none, the pipe carries bare frames as described here.

## Routes and roles

| Route | Role | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GET /api/ndi` | view | Whether the box is set up for NDI (`setup`, with the command when it is not), the runtime (present, version, a problem in words), the helper, the sources found, the saved addresses, what plays and its counts |
| `POST /api/ndi` `{"action": "add_address", "address": "192.168.1.20"}` | full | Also look for senders at this address |
| `POST /api/ndi` `{"action": "remove_address", "address": "..."}` | full | Forget it |
| `POST /api/ndi` `{"action": "sound", "on": true}` | full | Play a source's sound with its picture, or not |
| `POST /api/play` `{"ndi": "<id>"}` | live | Show a source. The id comes from `GET /api/ndi`; a name is never taken |

Settings: `"ndi": {"addresses": []}` and, once someone has used the switch, `"sound": true` or `false` (schema 15; 14 is the controller code's; sound needed no new schema, a file without the key means on). The switch is the module `inputs-ndi` (off by default, D43).

**On a box that is not set up** the routes answer cleanly and at once: `GET /api/ndi` gives `"setup": false`, `"helper": false`, no sources and the command; the addresses can be added and removed (they are plain settings, kept for when the box is set up); `POST /api/play` with an NDI source answers 409, "NDI is not set up on this box", with the command. The helper's socket is never tried there.

**Remote support: the same as Streams** (the owner's answer of 2026-10-08). A full-access support session may read and change the NDI addresses through the tunnel exactly as it may change Streams: neither route is on the list of what support can never do (`support.REMOTE_DENY`), neither handler has a rule of its own for the tunnel, and both ask the same roles. `tests/test_ndi_setup.py` (`TunnelSameAsStreamsTest`) pins that the two stay the same. What a support session cannot do is set NDI up or put a library on the box: that is root at the box, for everyone.

## Safety

Everything that arrives from the network is treated as hostile, and the code that reads it is a closed-source library, so the design assumes the helper can be taken over by a bad packet and limits what that would give.

- **Opt-in.** A box where nobody ran the command has no helper, no account for it, and nothing of NDI on the network. What follows is about a box that was set up.
- **Its own account and group** (`pvj-ndi`). It is not in group `pvj`, so it cannot reach the player's control socket (mpv's control channel can run programs), the PIN, the settings or the media. The player and the panel are each given the extra group `pvj-ndi` (by the two drop-ins the opt-in writes), which lets them read the helper's folder and nothing else. A unit that names a group the box does not have is not started by systemd, which is why the group is in drop-ins and not in the units the installer writes for every box.
- **Its sandbox** (`pvj/systemd/pvj-ndi.service`): no capabilities, no devices, a read-only system, no home folders, a `/tmp` of its own held to 8 MB, one writable folder (`/run/pvj-ndi`), at most 512 MB of memory (what it writes to `/run` counts), 64 tasks and three of a Pi 4's four cores (so the player always has one; figures chosen by reasoning, none measured), and **network addresses limited to private ranges, link-local and multicast** (`IPAddressDeny=any` with an allow list), which is meant to keep the library from talking to the internet at all. `tests/test_units.py` pins every line; that the lines do on a device what they say is step N9 of the device steps, not yet done.
- **The helper answers only the panel's account** on its socket (`SO_PEERCRED`), with the same small server the other helpers use.
- **The panel does not trust the helper's answers.** One deadline bounds a whole question and answer, however slowly the answer trickles in; an answer nested deeper than a real one is refused before it is read; every name, number and sentence in it is checked again (and scrubbed of control and direction-changing characters) before it reaches the page; and no answer, or fault, of the helper can stop the panel from starting.
- **Off means no NDI code running.** When the module is switched off after having been on, the helper ends and systemd starts a fresh one that loads nothing until the module is on again.
- **What ends the helper, and how often it is started again.** The unit starts it again 2 seconds after it ends, without a limit. Two things end it: the module being switched off with the library loaded (on purpose, see above), and a crash inside the library. A library that is missing, or that does not load, ends nothing: the helper stays up, loads nothing and says what is wrong on the page. A library that crashes while loading would be started again each time the panel asks for it, which is while somebody has the NDI page open with the module on; each start is small and bounded by the unit's limits, and it writes a line or two to the journal (kept, 64 MB at most). The 2 seconds were left as they are on purpose: a slower restart would also slow the clean start after switching off, and a different treatment by exit status cannot tell a crash in a closed library from anything else. If device step N3 shows the library crashing on load, switch the module off (that stops the asking) and send the journal.
- **The system bus stays reachable** from the helper, and has to: NDI's library finds senders through `libavahi-client`, which talks to `avahi-daemon` over D-Bus and no other way.
- **The pipe.** Only the helper's service makes and removes it. Before the player is told to read it, the panel looks that it is a pipe and belongs to the helper's account (a look that can be raced, so a tripwire, not a guarantee; the guarantee is that the player reads whatever is there as raw video only). mpv's own source (`stream/stream_file.c`, read on 2026-10-07 from its master branch) opens a file for reading without blocking and waits for data in a way a later command interrupts, so a helper that never writes does not hold the player; whether the older mpv on Raspberry Pi OS Bookworm (0.35) does the same was not determined and is step N11.
- **Names**: a source name from the network is shown and used only if it is valid UTF-8, 1 to 128 characters, with no control, format (bidirectional overrides, zero width) or line-separator characters. It is written one way (accented letters composed, every kind of space as a plain one), so two rows cannot look alike while being different, and a pile of accents on one letter is refused. At most 64 sources are kept. The panel plays by an id, a hash of the name and the announcing machine made by the helper, never by a string from the request.
- **Frames**: width, height, line stride and format are checked against fixed bounds (16 to 3840 by 16 to 2160, an even width, a stride of at least two bytes per pixel and at most a bounded excess) before a single byte is read from the library's buffer; the byte count read is computed from the checked values only.
- **Addresses** added by hand are IPv4 literals in a private range, at most 16, and are stored exactly as checked. The library is never given a host name or a string with a comma.
- **The library file** can only be put in place by root on the box, and the helper can only be set up by root on the box, with the same command.

What is NOT covered: anyone on the show network can announce a sender, with any name, including the name of a real one. Keep the show network private, as for streams and OSC.

### Why there is no upload button

A library is a program. A route that took the file from a browser would let any full-access phone run its own code on the box, which nothing in the panel can do today (updates are signed). A root command keeps that line where it is. The page says so and shows the command.

## Discovery and the network

- The library finds senders by mDNS through `avahi-daemon` (it talks to the daemon over the system D-Bus socket; the sandbox allows Unix sockets for that). mDNS is UDP port 5353, multicast, and does not cross routers or many Wi-Fi access points.
- Where mDNS does not pass, add the sender's IPv4 address on the page. The library then asks that machine directly (TCP 5960) for its sources.
- Video arrives on TCP or UDP ports from 5960 upward (5961 and up per stream, 6960 and 7960 and up for the multi-connection modes). The box runs no firewall of its own, so nothing needs opening on it. A managed switch or a firewall between sender and box must let these through.
- The helper only receives. It never announces a source.
- **A box that did not opt in shows nothing of NDI on the network**: there is no helper to ask for senders or to receive from one, and the installer adds no avahi for it. (This replaces the earlier design, in which the helper was installed idle on every box and the installer added `avahi-daemon` where it was missing.) Raspberry Pi OS has `avahi-daemon` anyway, and a box with it answers to its name by mDNS; that is the same with and without this project. On a box that is set up, the helper is idle (no library loaded, nothing sent) until the module is switched on. Device step N17 checks both on the wire.

Neither is a Pi.
- **The real library on Linux, beyond one run.** On 2026-10-08, once, on a Pi 4: `libndi.so.6` loaded under the sandbox, discovery worked from inside the unit, and progressive UYVY frames of two senders were received and played ("First run on a Pi 4"). Not shown: other senders and sizes, 60 frames a second, HX, alpha, a long run, or anything by eye.
- **A still source on the screen**: whether mpv draws the one frame a still sender gives through the raw pipe, and whether writing it a second time is what makes it do so (step N12). The first run says mpv did not advance past frame 0 with one frame; nobody saw the monitor.
- **An interlaced sender**: which frame types really arrive and that they are refused as written (step N13).
- **The bounds in the unit** (memory, tasks, processor, the small `/tmp`), and discovery without localhost (step N14).
- **Sound, on any box.** Nobody has heard it. Unknown: the delay; whether sound and picture are in step and stay so over minutes; what mpv does when a block is left out or silence is filled in (a click, a pause, nothing); the level of a film against a clip; the cost on a Pi 4; mpv 0.35.

- **Every sandbox line** in `pvj-ndi.service`, above all `IPAddressDeny`/`IPAddressAllow` (it needs the kernel's cgroup network filter), `PrivateDevices` and `RestrictAddressFamilies`. NDI HX is H.264 or HEVC decoded by the library in software and may be heavier still. Latency is unmeasured.
- A Pi 3 is not offered (the module lists Pi 4, Pi 5 and x86).
- The browser test step for the page was written and syntax-checked, not run (the dev Mac has no Playwright). Neither was the installer on the dev Mac: its tests (`tests/test_install.py`) need GNU tools and run in CI only, as does the real install under systemd (`tests/real_install_test.sh`).

## Open design questions

- **Sound: decided and built on 2026-10-08**, option (b) of the three that were weighed here (a second pipe of raw samples; one stream that holds both with times; leaving sound to the room): one stream with times, as Matroska and not NUT because mpv reads Matroska itself and the writer is forty lines. See "Sound". Open: the level of program senders, lip sync and delay on a box, and whether the first two channels are the right ones for the senders the owner uses.
- **Low bandwidth.** The library can ask a sender for its small proxy stream; a "small picture" choice per source would make a Pi 3 or a weak Wi-Fi link usable. Not built.
- **A source as a pad, in a scene or in the schedule.** The id is a hash of the name, so it stays the same across restarts and could be saved. Not built.
- **Getting the library onto a box without a keyboard.** A USB stick route (root's USB unit copies `ndi/libndi.so.6` from a stick) would suit a venue; physical access is already the box. Not built; needs the licence read first.

## Alternatives that were rejected

- **mpv or ffmpeg with NDI built in.** Debian's and Raspberry Pi OS's ffmpeg have no NDI (as far as is remembered, ffmpeg removed its NDI device in 2019 after a licence dispute; this was not looked up for this work), so it would mean shipping our own ffmpeg and mpv linked against a proprietary library.
- **The library inside the player or the panel.** A crash would take the screen down (the reason the capture input has a helper), and a takeover would have the PIN, the settings and the stream passwords (panel) or the screen and the control socket (player).
- **The helper as a child of the panel**, like the capture helper. It would inherit the panel's account and its access to the settings.
- **A compiled helper** (C, with or without GStreamer's NDI plugin). Faster to a known-good result, but it breaks "standard library only" and adds a build per board. If the Pi 4 measurement shows Python's copy is the bottleneck, this is the next thing to weigh, and it is the owner's call.
- **YUV4MPEG instead of raw frames.** It carries the size in a header, but only planar formats, so every frame would be rearranged in Python.
- **An upload route for the library.** See above.
